# Sample Short-Form Video Teardown: hooks, narration pace and editing style of high-performing faceless Shorts (as of 18 November 2026)

> **Read this first: how the samples were "watched", and the limits that follow**
> - **I could not watch or download any video directly.** In this session the egress proxy refused `www.youtube.com` and `www.tiktok.com` (HTTP 403 on CONNECT; the proxy README has no per-tool fix, and it says not to route around a policy denial). WebFetch could not resolve any host at all, so blog teardowns (Substack, vidIQ, beehiiv) were unreachable too. Only WebSearch summaries and public GitHub (read-only git) worked.
> - **What I measured instead:** I found three public, machine-readable datasets on GitHub that other people recorded from the actual video files with yt-dlp and ffmpeg. I then **recomputed the metrics myself** from their raw files with my own scripts (`python3 -I`, scratchpad only):
>   1. **Faceless explainer set (16 Shorts used here).** It comes from a creator's competitor-research folder, pulled around 20 Oct 2026, and holds per-video yt-dlp metadata, YouTube auto-caption files with word-level timestamps (json3), and ffmpeg scene-cut and loudness logs. From these I computed WPM, time to first word, the verbatim spoken hook in the first 3 s, the closing words (for loop detection), hard cuts per 10 s and mean volume. — [Peiush/Yt-channel-folders, competitors/raw/media](https://github.com/Peiush/Yt-channel-folders/tree/main/yt-channel/edge-of-alive-research/work/competitors/raw/media)
>   2. **Big-creator hook study (167 Reels, TikToks and Shorts, measured 17 Nov 2026).** It covers the time of the first cut, cuts per minute, speech within 2 s and text on the first frame. I used it for the high-energy contrasts and the platform baselines. — [shortformkit/short-form-video-hook-study-2026](https://github.com/shortformkit/short-form-video-hook-study-2026)
>   3. **Delivery and loudness study (135 videos, 12 Oct 2026).** It gives LUFS and true peak as each platform serves the file. — [shortformkit/short-form-video-delivery-2026](https://github.com/shortformkit/short-form-video-delivery-2026)
> - **Visual descriptions are second-hand.** Art style, caption look and beat timing come from a third party's 1 fps contact-sheet notes ([frame_notes.md](https://github.com/Peiush/Yt-channel-folders/blob/main/yt-channel/edge-of-alive-research/work/competitors/raw/frame_notes.md), [06_video_breakdowns.md](https://github.com/Peiush/Yt-channel-folders/blob/main/yt-channel/edge-of-alive-research/06_video_breakdowns.md)). For the #1 sample there is also a frame-accurate beat sheet measured from the 930-frame file ([az9713/stitches-blender-remake beats.md](https://github.com/az9713/stitches-blender-remake/blob/main/beats.md)). **I did not view any frames myself and saved no frame images.** No audio was listened to, by me or by these sources. Sound notes are ffmpeg measurements only.
> - No media was downloaded. The cloned repos were reduced to text files, and nothing was written into the toolkit repo except this note.

## Per-video teardown (table): channel, title, URL, upload date, views, length, WPM, cuts, first visual change, hooks, captions, style, music, beats, payoff and ending

### Takeaway
I measured 20 Shorts. Sixteen are faceless explainers, and the eight largest have 33M–255M views. Four are high-energy face-on contrasts, with 0.8M–83M views. The faceless winners narrate at about **145–178 WPM** and say their first word within **≤0.16 s**. They speak a conditional "If/When you…" premise in the first 3 s (about 8–12 words). The biggest of them (Zack D. Films, 138M–255M views) use **almost no hard cuts**: one continuous camera move with something animating from frame 0. Faster faceless channels (Low Poly, Helix²) change shot every ~1.5–3 s and narrate at 200–230 WPM.

### Cited Findings

**Method notes for the tables**
- **WPM** is ASR words ÷ video duration × 60. The figure in brackets uses the speech span (first to last word). YouTube auto-captions can drop words, so treat WPM as a slight under-estimate. — computed from [json3 captions + meta](https://github.com/Peiush/Yt-channel-folders/tree/main/yt-channel/edge-of-alive-research/work/competitors/raw/media)
- **Hard cuts/10 s** come from `ffmpeg select='gt(scene,0.3)'` run on 240×426 downloads. **Visible shots/10 s** are the third party's count by eye between 1 fps frames. That count is a lower bound, and the third party warns that ffmpeg "badly under-counts 3D cuts". — [scenes.txt files](https://github.com/Peiush/Yt-channel-folders/tree/main/yt-channel/edge-of-alive-research/work/competitors/raw/media); [06_video_breakdowns §5](https://github.com/Peiush/Yt-channel-folders/blob/main/yt-channel/edge-of-alive-research/06_video_breakdowns.md)
- **Views** for rows 1–16 are as of the third party's pull (~20 Oct 2026). Rows 17–20 are as of 17 Nov 2026. **Mean vol** is ffmpeg `volumedetect` mean dBFS of the full mix (voice and music not separated). "[music] tags" counts how often YouTube's own ASR inserted `[music]`. — [media folder](https://github.com/Peiush/Yt-channel-folders/tree/main/yt-channel/edge-of-alive-research/work/competitors/raw/media); [hook-study videos.csv](https://github.com/shortformkit/short-form-video-hook-study-2026/blob/main/data/videos.csv)

## Patterns: what do the top calm/faceless examples share; how do slow-narrated videos keep visuals moving; rhythm of visual changes; caption styling; hook length and first-payoff timing?

### Takeaway
The top faceless Shorts share five traits:
1. A second-person conditional premise is spoken from frame 0, about 8–12 words in the first 3 s.
2. Something is already **moving** in the first frame.
3. A concrete, visual process unfolds with no dead air. Audio is continuous, with no silences ≥0.4 s in 25 of 28 videos.
4. They run 24–41 s.
5. They end on a resolution image, a punchline or a loop, never a spoken CTA.

The very biggest replace cuts with continuous camera choreography (push-in → hold → creep → fast push → pull-out back to frame 0). Captions are usually 1–3 white bold words at lower-middle, but the #1 video (255M) has none.

### Inferences
- **How slow-narrated videos keep visuals moving.** They never hold a static frame. Each spoken clause gets a new visual state (an object lands, swells, grows, is labelled), and between clauses the virtual camera drifts or pushes. Stitches changes visual state roughly every 1–4 s with zero cuts. For 2D vector or hand-drawn work, the equivalents are a slow scale or pan on every shot, elements drawing or popping in on the stressed word, and cutaway/X-ray reveals.
- **Hook length.** The spoken hook is one clause of ~8–12 words, finished by ~2.5–3 s. The visual hook lands in the first second: motion at frame 0, or a gross or surprising image by 1 s (Eye Socket), or a preview of the extreme end-state (Helix²).
- **First-payoff timing has two tiers.** A *micro-payoff*, the first concrete answer, instruction or reveal, lands at ~1.5–6 s (Stabbed "Don't panic" 1.5 s, Ice 2–5 s, Sleep reveal 5–6 s, Low Poly 5–7 s). The *main payoff* lands at ~55–75% of runtime (Splinters ~17 of 30 s, Stitches scar ~26 of 31 s). Rows 5 and 2 add a mid-video re-hook ("But if you…", "but sometimes…") at ~3–11 s.
- **Captions are optional when the visual alone explains everything** (Stitches). For a calm channel whose value is the tip itself, captions are still the safer default: 18 of 26 used them, and the content is "useful info" that viewers may want to read.

### Gaps
- None of the measured samples is a deadpan, hand-drawn or whiteboard Short with timing data, so the patterns are drawn mostly from 3D explainers. Whether the same rhythms hold for 2D doodles is inference.
- The faceless sample is chosen for winners and is skewed toward body and survival topics. The third party notes that counts are "descriptive, not proof".
- Retention curves for these competitors are not public. "Works" here means high views, not measured retention.

## Calm examples vs high-energy contrasts: what differs, and what can a calm channel borrow without losing its tone?

### Takeaway
High-energy big creators cut about **4× as often** as calm talkers: a median of ~46 vs ~11 cuts/min on YouTube, or ~7.7 vs ~1.8 per 10 s. They make the first cut earlier (2.25 s vs 4.9 s) and rarely put text on the first frame. The calm mega-hits cut even less (0 hard cuts) but match the energetic group on **speed to premise**: words start within 0.2 s, motion in frame 0 and payoff early. A calm channel should borrow the *front-loading* (instant speech, a moving first frame, a micro-payoff by ~5 s, a mid-video re-hook, a tag or loop ending), not the cut rate or a faster voice.

### Cited Findings
- **High-energy YouTube creators** in the hook study (MrBeast, Alan's Universe, Airrack, Stokes Twins, Max the Meat Guy, Joshua Weissman, Nick DiGiovanni; n=21 Shorts): median first cut 2.25 s, median **46.1 cuts/min**, speech within 2 s in 17 of 21, text on the first frame in only 5 of 21. **Calm talkers** (Marques Brownlee, Jay Shetty, Alex Hormozi; n=8): median first cut 4.9 s, **10.7 cuts/min**, text on the first frame in 6 of 8. **All 59 YouTube Shorts:** first cut 2.53 s, 25.4 cuts/min. — computed from [hook-study videos.csv](https://github.com/shortformkit/short-form-video-hook-study-2026/blob/main/data/videos.csv)
- **Platform baselines (167 videos):** median first cut 2.6 s; only 14% cut within 1 s; median shot 2.3 s (TikTok 3.1 s); 95% have sound from the first frame; 84% of spoken videos speak within 2 s; 58% show edited-in text on the first frame. — [hook study README](https://github.com/shortformkit/short-form-video-hook-study-2026)
- **Alan's Universe** cuts at 0.63–1.3 s, with 3 cuts in the first 3 s and 37.7–53.5 cuts/min. **MrBeast's 83M-view Short:** first cut 2.34 s, 44.9 cuts/min. **Ryan Trahan's 28M Short:** 26.4 cuts/min. — [hook-study videos.csv](https://github.com/shortformkit/short-form-video-hook-study-2026/blob/main/data/videos.csv)
- **Loudness.** MrBeast's YouTube Shorts measured a median of −10.9 LUFS. YouTube Shorts overall had a median of −19.6 LUFS, because YouTube keeps the uploaded level. TikTok does not level delivered files (median −21.2). Instagram normalises to about −14 LUFS. — [delivery study](https://github.com/shortformkit/short-form-video-delivery-2026) The calm ZDF Stitches mix is −12.0 LUFS integrated with a narrow 1.6 LU loudness range. — [beats.md](https://github.com/az9713/stitches-blender-remake/blob/main/beats.md)
- **Faceless "fast" vs "calm" in the same niche.** Low Poly, Helix² and His Story run 202–243 WPM at ~3.5–6.4 shots per 10 s. ZDF's body mega-hits run 144–177 WPM at 0–2.2 shots per 10 s. Both reach tens of millions of views. — computed from [json3 captions](https://github.com/Peiush/Yt-channel-folders/tree/main/yt-channel/edge-of-alive-research/work/competitors/raw/media); [06_video_breakdowns §5](https://github.com/Peiush/Yt-channel-folders/blob/main/yt-channel/edge-of-alive-research/06_video_breakdowns.md)
- **"Brainrot" / Reddit-story format (qualitative, vendor sources only).** The signature is a split screen of TTS narration over continuous mobile gameplay (Subway Surfers), with word-by-word captions, and vendors stress caption sync. An often-quoted "80% watch with sound off" figure is unsourced vendor material. — [GhostShorts](https://ghostshorts.com/blog/how-to-make-reddit-story-videos-2026); [Study Breaks](https://studybreaks.com/tvfilm/subway-surfers-new-storytelling-method)
- **TikTok's own creative guidance:** over 63% of the highest-CTR ad videos put their key message or product in the first 3 s. This comes from ads, gives no published method, and is correlational. The "88% say sound is vital" figure traces to a 2022 Kantar user survey, not to measured sound-on rates. — [TikTok Auction Ads Creative Tips (PDF)](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/library/Auction_Ads_Creative_Tips.pdf) (via search summary; PDF not opened)

### Inferences
- **Borrow without losing tone:**
  - (a) Speech from frame 0, a premise by 3 s, and motion in the first frame. This matches both groups.
  - (b) A micro-payoff by ~5 s.
  - (c) A mid-video pivot line ("But here's the thing…" / "But if you…").
  - (d) One visible change per narration clause. "Events" (pop-ins, label reveals, zooms) can stand in for cuts.
  - (e) A dry comic tag or a loop line at the end.
  - (f) Text on the first frame, which calm talkers use far more than energetic creators (6 of 8 vs 5 of 21). This suggests text compensates when the picture alone is less arresting.
- **Do not borrow:** sub-second cut chains (Alan's Universe), 200+ WPM delivery, shouting, or loud meme SFX. These are the "energy" markers. A deadpan AI voice at 150–170 WPM is within the measured range of the calm hits.
- The brainrot device of "constant background motion under narration" maps to a calm channel as a slow, continuous ambient animation layer (drifting doodles, a gently moving background) under the explainer. That keeps the eye busy without fast cuts.

### Gaps
- No measured WPM or spoken hooks exist for the high-energy contrasts. The hook study publishes only flags, and YouTube was unreachable.
- No measured brainrot or Reddit-story Short was available. Its characterisation rests on vendor blogs.
- Whether the calm format's lower cut rate holds on TikTok specifically is untested here. The platform-wide data shows TikTok has the calmest median shot length (3.1 s).

## Editing-style spec for this channel (derived): cut rhythm, captions, hook, sound design, ending/loop, and a first-3-seconds checklist

### Takeaway
Here is a measured-range spec for a calm, deadpan, faceless 2D Short of 25–45 s:
- Narration at **150–170 WPM** (≈2.5–2.8 words/s), first word at ≤0.15 s.
- A conditional "If you…/You've probably…" premise of ≤12 words finished by 3 s.
- A moving first frame.
- A visual state change every ~1–3 s, with hard cuts optional (target ~1–3 shots per 10 s plus continuous camera drift).
- 1–3-word captions at lower-middle.
- Continuous audio mastered to about −14 LUFS.
- A loop-line or dry-tag ending with no spoken CTA.

All numbers below are **inferences** from the cited measurements above, not platform rules.

### Inferences (the spec)

**1. Runtime and pacing**
- Target 25–45 s. The ZDF hits are 30–40 s, and the median of the measured faceless set is 35 s.
- Script about 70–110 words at 150–170 WPM. Example: 32 s × 2.6 words/s ≈ 83 words, which is what the Stitches remake team targeted ("82 words, target 2.6 words/s"). — [beats.md](https://github.com/az9713/stitches-blender-remake/blob/main/beats.md)
- Deadpan does not have to mean slow. Keep word rate in range and get the deadpan from flat intonation and short declaratives.
- Map Hook → Why → What → How → Solution to time:

  | Section | Time |
  |---|---|
  | Hook | 0–3 s |
  | Why | 3–8 s, including a micro-payoff or "but…" pivot |
  | What | 8–15 s |
  | How | 15–25 s, the main mechanism or steps |
  | Solution / payoff | at ~60–80% of runtime |
  | Tag / loop line | last 2–3 s |

**2. Visual-change rhythm (2D vector or hand-drawn)**
- Something moves at frame 0: a hand mid-motion, an object falling, a line drawing itself.
- A new visual state (pop-in, label, zoom target, cutaway) on every narration clause, roughly every 1–3 s.
- Hard cuts or scene changes: ~1–3 per 10 s. That sits between the ZDF mega-hits (0–2.2) and the big-creator median (~4.2).
- A continuous slow camera push or drift on every shot (Stitches pushes 1.0 → 1.43× over 4 s).
- One faster "accent" move (snap zoom or whip pan) at the main reveal, matching Stitches' fastest push at 14–16 s.
- Avoid holds longer than ~2 s with no motion.
- Use an X-ray or cutaway, or a before/after split, for the "How" section.

**3. Caption spec**
- 1–3 words per caption, changing on phrase boundaries (~every 0.7–1.2 s).
- White bold rounded sans with a dark outline or soft shadow.
- Lower-middle, around 60–75% of frame height, to stay clear of the platform UI. *The exact safe zone was not measured here.*
- One accent colour for the key word (How to Survive Show used red; Human Buddy a purple box).
- Cut-on/cut-off timing for a deadpan feel. Skip bouncy pop animations, which read as high-energy (an inference).
- The first caption is on screen at 0.0 s.
- An optional small title card for the first ~2–3 s, which Kurzgesagt and Astrum use.

**4. Hook spec (first 1.5–3 s)**
- **Spoken:** one second-person conditional or yes/no clause, ≤12 words, starting ≤0.15 s. Examples: "If you…, you're probably…", "When you…", "Could you…?"
- **On-screen text:** either the first caption words, or a ≤6-word title card restating the premise or a surprising number.
- **Visual:** the problem object already in motion, plus one surprise. Options are an impossible or gross image by ~1 s, or a 0.5–1 s flash-forward of the end-state (Helix²'s "DAY 7").

**5. Sound design**
- Voice and music from frame 0, with no silent gaps of 0.4 s or more. Use pauses under ~0.4 s for deadpan comic timing.
- Master to about −14 LUFS integrated (matches Instagram's normalisation; louder than the YouTube median), with true peak ≤ −1 dBTP and a narrow loudness range, as Stitches has (LRA 1.6 LU).
- Keep the music bed low and steady. YouTube's ASR tagged no `[music]` in any ZDF mega-hit, which suggests the music never competes with the voice. The exact bed level could not be measured.
- SFX: soft and diegetic (pop, snip, pencil scratch, "ding") on visual reveals. No meme SFX. No measured data supports this; it is a tone-matching inference.

**6. Ending and loop spec**
- No spoken CTA.
- End on either:
  - (a) a **loop line**: the last sentence is grammatically completed by the first ("…which is exactly why" → "If you…", as Astrum does), with the final frame matching frame 0 (as Stitches does); or
  - (b) a **dry tag**: a one-line deadpan aside after the solution (Low Poly, Zeck Felms, Kurzgesagt).
- Since replays count as views, a loop ending inflates public views. Engaged views, the monetization metric, are unaffected.

**7. First-3-seconds checklist**
1. First spoken word at ≤0.15 s; no greeting or logo.
2. The premise is understandable from audio alone by 3 s (≤12 words, says "you").
3. The premise is understandable with sound off by 3 s (caption or title card visible from 0.0 s).
4. Frame 0 already shows motion and the subject. No blank or fade-in.
5. One surprising or specific detail is visible or heard by ~1–1.5 s (a number, an odd image, a contradiction of "most people").
6. Audio bed and voice start together at 0.0 s.
7. The first visual change (cut, zoom accent or new element) lands by ~1–2.5 s. The big-creator median first cut is 2.6 s, and the Stitches push-in moves continuously from 0 s.
8. The micro-payoff or "but…" pivot is queued for ≤6 s.
9. The last line of the script was checked to flow back into this opening line.

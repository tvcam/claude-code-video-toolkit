## 1. How each algorithm treats new accounts and which signals matter (YouTube Shorts, TikTok, plus cross-posting effects)

### Takeaway
Both platforms judge each short **per video, not per channel**. They show it to a small test audience first, then widen distribution if early viewers watch it instead of swiping away and keep watching to the end. Neither platform publishes test-batch sizes or pass thresholds; the "200–500 viewers" and "70% viewed" numbers are creator folklore. Since **31 March 2025**, YouTube counts a Shorts "view" every time a Short starts or replays. Monetization and YPP eligibility still use the older **engaged views** metric. Deleting and reposting is officially discouraged on YouTube. Instagram has said since 2021 that it avoids recommending Reels that carry another app's watermark.

### Cited Findings
**YouTube Shorts: view counting and metrics**
- [PRESS] From 31 March 2025, YouTube Shorts "views" count every time a Short starts to play or replays, with no minimum watch time. This matches how TikTok and Instagram count — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/03/26/youtube-is-changing-how-youtube-shorts-views-are-counted); [eMarketer](https://emarketer.com/content/youtube-shorts-changes-view-count-rules-match-tiktok--instagram)
- [PRESS] The previous metric was kept and renamed **"engaged views."** It is visible only in YouTube Analytics Advanced Mode. YouTube said the change "will not directly impact" YPP eligibility or Shorts ad revenue sharing, and both remain based on engaged views — [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/youtube-changes-shorts-view-counts-no-change-to-monetization/543005/); [Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2025/03/26/youtube-shorts-views-counting-stats/)
- [PRESS] Brands welcomed the change. Creators were skeptical and expected RPM per (new-style) view to fall as view totals rose — [Digiday](https://digiday.com/media/youtube-shorts-view-count-update-wins-over-brands-but); [Entrepreneur](https://www.entrepreneur.com/business-news/youtube-shorts-will-see-more-view-counts-earnings/489190)
- [BLOG] "Viewed vs. swiped away" is a real YouTube Studio metric: the share of Shorts-feed impressions where the viewer chose to watch rather than scroll past. It is measured *before* the watch. A very new Short, or one whose traffic came mostly from subscriptions, the channel page, search or external links, may have too few feed impressions to show a percentage — [Grow Creator](https://growcreator.pro/blog/viewed-vs-swiped-away-youtube-shorts)
- [BLOG] Seed-audience testing is widely described: every Short goes to a small seed audience first, and distribution stops if retention is low or swipe-away is high. YouTube has **never published cohort sizes or thresholds**, and specific "kill thresholds" (e.g., 70% viewed) are invented rules of thumb — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-shorts-algorithm/); [Grow Creator](https://growcreator.pro/blog/viewed-vs-swiped-away-youtube-shorts); [ContentMatch](https://contentmatch.io/youtube-shorts-algorithm-viewed-vs-swiped-ratio-2026/)
- [BLOG] YouTube is described as evaluating Shorts per video and per topic, not channel-wide, and as treating Shorts and long-form audiences separately — [Grow Creator](https://growcreator.pro/blog/viewed-vs-swiped-away-youtube-shorts)

**YouTube: posting volume, deleting and reposting (official statements, 2023–2024)**
- [OFFICIAL, via press] YouTube Shorts product lead Todd Sherman: "There's nothing in the algorithm that dictates expanded or reduced reach if you post more Shorts." He added that posting lots of lower-quality clips can have negative effects — [Benzinga](https://benzinga.com/z/34020532); [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/youtube-explains-how-shorts-algorithm-works/494953/)
- [OFFICIAL, via press] Sherman also said that deleting and re-uploading Shorts "to try to freshen up leftovers can also result in downgrades and potentially get a clip labeled as spam" — [Benzinga](https://benzinga.com/z/34020532); [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/youtube-advice-for-shorts-creators-hashtags-algorithm/691835/)
- [OFFICIAL] Creator Liaison Rene Ritchie's roundup repeated Sherman's points on "why some Shorts flatline, #hashtags, and why you shouldn't delete and repost." Ritchie also advised: "Think twice before deleting, privating, or unlisting any videos purely because you think it'll change your channel's performance" — [YouTube Blog, Rene's Top Five, 18 Apr 2024](https://blog.youtube/culture-and-trends/renes-top-five-on-youtube-april-18-2024-edition/)

**TikTok For You feed**
- [BLOG] New videos are said to go first to a small test audience, with estimates of 100–500 or 200–500 users, chosen by language, country, device, time zone and interest match. Videos that clear an engagement bar are shown to progressively larger groups. **These numbers are not confirmed by TikTok** — [Conbersa](https://www.conbersa.ai/learn/tiktok-recommendation-algorithm-2026); [Foundation](https://foundationinc.co/lab/for-you-page/); [Darkroom](https://www.darkroomagency.com/observatory/how-tiktok%E2%80%99s-algorithm-works-in-2026-and-15-tactics-to-go-viral)
- [BLOG] Signal priority as described by third parties: completion rate (% watched) and rewatches first, with shares ranked above comments and likes. One blog claims a ~70% completion threshold for wider distribution; this is unverified and blogs disagree — [Conbersa](https://www.conbersa.ai/learn/tiktok-recommendation-algorithm-2026); [Darkroom](https://www.darkroomagency.com/observatory/how-tiktok%E2%80%99s-algorithm-works-in-2026-and-15-tactics-to-go-viral)
- [BLOG, attributing to TikTok] TikTok has reportedly said that follower count and a creator's past video performance are *not* direct For You feed ranking factors. The secondary source did not link the TikTok document, so this could not be verified — [Foundation](https://foundationinc.co/lab/for-you-page/)
- [PRESS] Since Nov 2025, TikTok users can choose how much AI-generated content they see in their feed (a "Manage topics" style control). This adds a viewer-side lever that can reduce distribution of AI-labeled videos — [TechCrunch, 18 Nov 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/11/18/tiktok-now-lets-you-choose-how-much-ai-generated-content-you-want-to-see/)

**Cross-posting and watermarks (Reels, Shorts)**
- [OFFICIAL, via press] Instagram head Adam Mosseri: Instagram tries not to recommend Reels carrying logos or watermarks from other apps, but a creator's *own* logo is fine. The policy dates to Feb 2021 — [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-clarifies-including-your-own-logo-on-a-reel-is-ok/730852/); [Social Media Today 2021](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-will-now-limit-the-reach-of-re-posts-from-tiktok-within-its-reels/594803); [Tubefilter 2021](https://www.tubefilter.com/2021/02/10/instagram-reels-with-tiktok-watermark-less-discoverable/amp/)
- [BLOG] Meta's newer account-level unoriginal-content rule reportedly took effect **30 April 2026**. Accounts that "primarily post unoriginal reels, photos, or carousels you didn't create or edit in a material way" may stop appearing in recommendations. No Meta primary source was found in this session — [CreatorStack](https://creatorstack-io.pages.dev/blog/meta-original-content-rules-demonetization-2026); [Lumina Clippers](https://luminaclippers.com/blog/instagram-unoriginal-content-policy)
- [BLOG] Claims that a TikTok logo cuts Reels reach by "~90%" are unsourced and likely exaggerated — [Search summary of watermark guides, e.g. XDA](https://www.xda-developers.com/psa-do-not-upload-tiktok-instagram-reels-without-removing-watermark/)

### Inferences
- New accounts get no special "boost" or "penalty" that has been confirmed. Each upload is effectively a fresh test, so the first 2 seconds (swipe decision) and the full-watch/rewatch rate matter far more than channel size. Deadpan tips should open on the payoff or the odd fact, not a slow setup.
- Because YouTube views now count starts and replays, **public view counts will look much higher than engaged views**. Track *engaged views* (Advanced Mode) for monetization progress.
- Cross-post rule: export a **clean master file with no platform watermark** from the editor (Remotion render) and upload that same clean file natively to each platform. Do not download a TikTok and re-upload it to Reels or Shorts. Your own channel logo or handle burned in is fine on Instagram.
- Never delete and repost an underperforming Short or TikTok. Make a *new* edit with a different hook instead. YouTube explicitly says reposting risks spam labeling.
- A clearly original, consistent format matters for Meta: it is the creator's own work, so the April 2026 unoriginal rule should not bite.

### Gaps
- No official TikTok document on seed-batch size, completion thresholds, or "new account" treatment was retrievable; all TikTok specifics above are third-party.
- No official YouTube statement from 2025–2026 on Shorts seed testing was found (Todd Beaupré quotes were not surfaced).
- Whether TikTok's AI-content feed slider measurably reduces reach for TTS-narrated animation is unknown; no data found.
- Whether TikTok itself penalizes deleting and reposting has no official source (folklore only).

## 2. Evidence-based posting cadence (how often, and whether consistency beats volume)

### Takeaway
Large datasets show more weekly posts give more views per post on TikTok, and Buffer's data says consistency week-over-week matters even more than volume. Platforms themselves give no numeric target, except a widely repeated but unverified TikTok "1–4 posts/day" claim. YouTube says posting more does not change reach by itself, but low-quality volume hurts. A realistic, sustainable target for a solo faceless creator is **one new video per day (5–7/week) across both platforms**, with a floor of **3–5/week** that never lapses.

### Cited Findings
- [3P DATA] Buffer (11M+ TikTok posts): creators posting **2–5 times/week saw up to 17% more views per post**, and those posting **11+ times/week up to 34% more**, versus posting once a week. Gains flatten after that. One summary notes the high-frequency gain reflects more "lottery tickets", not a per-post algorithmic boost — [Buffer, How to Grow on Social Media in 2026](https://buffer.com/resources/creator-growth-playbook/); summarized by [SocialCrawl](https://www.socialcrawl.dev/blog/how-often-should-you-post-on-tiktok) and [PostEverywhere](https://posteverywhere.ai/blog/how-often-to-post-on-social-media)
- [3P DATA] Buffer (2M+ Instagram posts): the sweet spot is **3–5 posts/week**, with ~12% more reach per post than posting 1–2×/week — [Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/how-often-to-post-on-instagram/); [Egochi summary](https://www.egochi.com/blog/how-often-to-post-on-social-media/)
- [3P DATA] Buffer consistency finding: highly consistent posters (posting in at least 20 of 26 weeks) earned **~450% more engagement per post** than sporadic posters. Reported secondhand; full methodology not read — [PostEverywhere](https://posteverywhere.ai/blog/how-often-to-post-on-social-media); [Egochi](https://www.egochi.com/blog/how-often-to-post-on-social-media/)
- [3P DATA/BLOG] Buffer's YouTube guidance is a summary recommendation of **1–3 Shorts/week**, not a study result. YouTube's own guidance is non-numeric: "The best schedule is the one that works for you and your audience" — [PostEverywhere](https://posteverywhere.ai/blog/how-often-to-post-on-social-media)
- [OFFICIAL, via press] YouTube (Todd Sherman): posting more Shorts does not expand or reduce reach algorithmically, but lots of lower-quality clips can have negative effects — [Benzinga](https://benzinga.com/z/34020532)
- [BLOG, attributed to TikTok] TikTok's creator materials are widely said to recommend **1–4 posts/day**. No primary TikTok page confirmed this. Other sources say TikTok's Creator Portal emphasizes schedule consistency over volume — [Lilach Bullock](https://www.lilachbullock.com/how-many-times-a-day-should-you-post-on-tiktok/); [MegaDigital](https://megadigital.ai/en/blog/how-often-should-you-post-on-tiktok/); [Shortimize](https://shortimize.com/blog/how-often-to-post-on-tiktok)
- [3P DATA] vidIQ's study of 448,413 channels (July 2026) analysed channels that posted **at least 10 Shorts in their first 90 days**. Among these, 86.5% had one of their first ten Shorts hit 1,000 views within 30 days, which is a survivorship-filtered sample of active posters. vidIQ separately says hybrid (Shorts + long-form) channels tend to outperform single-format channels — [vidIQ Research](https://vidiq.com/research/youtube-first-10-videos-study/); [vidIQ via Rosetta](https://rosetta.to/u/vidiq/shorts-vs-longs-how-to-get-more-views-in-2026)
- [BLOG] Unverified claims such as "daily posting cuts per-Short views by 40%" circulate. No official or dataset support was found for them — [AIR Media-Tech](https://air.io/en/youtube-hacks/the-death-of-daily-uploads-what-cadence-actually-triggers-algorithm-love-in-2025)

### Inferences
- **Recommended cadence for the 30-day launch.** One new video per day, posted natively to TikTok and YouTube Shorts, with the same clean file cross-posted to Reels. A minimum of 5/week; weekends optional. This lands in Buffer's 2–5+/week band with headroom, and hits vidIQ's "≥10 Shorts in first 90 days" cohort inside two weeks.
- Do not chase 2–4/day. The evidence for >1/day is "more lottery tickets," and YouTube warns that low-quality volume can hurt. For a TTS + vector format, the inauthentic-content risk (section 5) also rises with templated volume.
- **Batch production.** Produce 7–10 videos per weekly batch and schedule them (section 10). This protects the "never miss a week" consistency signal that Buffer's data rewards most.

### Gaps
- Buffer's full methodology (account mix, whether brands or creators, how "views per post" were normalized) could not be read; the buffer.com fetch was blocked.
- No YouTube-Shorts-specific frequency dataset from Sprout, Hootsuite, Later, Metricool or Dash Hudson was found in this session.
- TikTok's "1–4/day" recommendation has no primary source found.

## 3. Best posting times and days for US audiences (TikTok, YouTube Shorts)

### Takeaway
The 2026 studies **disagree**. Sprout Social says weekday afternoons (Tue–Thu, 2–6 pm local). Buffer says weekend mornings for TikTok (Sun 9 am) and Friday late afternoon or evening for Shorts (4, 6, 7 pm). Most datasets are dominated by brand accounts, not Gen-Z-targeted creators. Use a starting window, then switch to each channel's own analytics after about 2–3 weeks of data.

### Cited Findings
- [3P DATA] Buffer 2026 (52M+ posts, all platforms): TikTok's top single slot is **Sunday 9 am**. Another Buffer summary also names Monday 1 pm, and Saturday is the highest-ranked day — [Buffer, Best Time to Post on Social Media in 2026](https://buffer.com/resources/best-time-to-post-social-media/); [IQFluence summary](https://iqfluence.io/public/blog/best-time-to-post-on-tiktok)
- [3P DATA] Buffer YouTube (1.8M videos): **Friday** performs best for Shorts, with **4 pm, 6 pm and 7 pm** as the top slots — [Buffer, Best Time to Post on YouTube](https://buffer.com/resources/best-time-to-post-on-youtube/)
- [3P DATA] Sprout Social 2026 (~2B engagements, ~307k profiles): TikTok's best times are **Tue–Thu, 2–6 pm in the audience's local time**, avoiding weekends — [Sprout Social](https://sproutsocial.com/insights/best-time-to-post-on-tiktok/)
- [3P DATA] Hootsuite (1M+ posts, 118 countries; reportedly a 2025 dataset): sources conflict, citing either Thu 6–9 am and Sat 10 am–6 pm, or Tue 9 am — [IQFluence](https://iqfluence.io/public/blog/best-time-to-post-on-tiktok); [eClincher](https://www.eclincher.com/articles/best-time-to-post-on-tiktok-in-2026-what-the-data-really-says)
- [BLOG] Evening-focused guides put the Shorts peak at 7–9 pm (8 pm on weekdays) and TikTok at 7–10 pm. These are not dataset-backed — [HypeNest](https://hypenest.ai/blogs/best-time-to-post-youtube-shorts-tiktok); [AutoShort](https://autoshort.io/blog/best-posting-times-2026)
- [3P DATA/BLOG] Consensus caveat: nearly every source, including those that disagree, says to use your own audience data (TikTok Analytics → Followers → follower activity; YouTube Studio → Audience → "When your viewers are on YouTube") and your audience's time zone. Consistency and quality are cited as mattering more than the exact hour — [Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/best-time-to-post-social-media/); [IQFluence](https://iqfluence.io/public/blog/best-time-to-post-on-tiktok)
- [3P DATA] Only secondary blogs give US-Eastern-specific tables, and they cite no primary data — [IQFluence](https://iqfluence.io/public/blog/best-time-to-post-on-tiktok)

### Inferences
- **Starter windows for a US Gen-Z audience (to be replaced by own analytics after ~3 weeks):**
  - Weekdays: **3–6 pm ET**, which is after school/class and overlaps Sprout's window and Buffer's Friday 4–7 pm Shorts slots. An evening test slot of 7–9 pm ET is an option.
  - Weekends: **9–11 am ET**, matching Buffer's Sunday 9 am TikTok peak.
  - A single daily slot such as 4 pm ET also catches 1 pm PT. Alternate a few posts to 7–8 pm ET in weeks 2–3 to A/B test.
- Because distribution is a staged test lasting hours to days, posting time affects mainly the first batch. It is a second-order lever next to hook quality.
- Teens 13–17 are in school on weekday daytimes during term, which supports afternoon/evening and weekend-morning slots. This is an inference, not from a dataset.

### Gaps
- No 2025–2026 dataset segmented by audience age (Gen Z) or by faceless/education niche was found.
- Dash Hudson, Later and Metricool 2026 best-time data were not surfaced.

## 4. Video length rules and monetization thresholds (YouTube Shorts 3-min, TikTok 1-min rule, YPP tiers, Shorts revenue)

### Takeaway
YouTube Shorts can run up to **3 minutes**. Observational data favors roughly **15–60 s** for views, and there is **no head-to-head evidence that 60–180 s Shorts get more reach**. TikTok's **Creator Rewards Program requires videos over 1 minute** plus 10k followers and 100k views in 30 days, so the planned sub-60 s format **cannot earn Creator Rewards**. **YPP changes on 1 Feb 2027**: new applicants will need **1,000 subs + 20M qualified Shorts views in 90 days** (or 8,000 watch hours in 365 days). Even after joining, a channel needs **10M qualified Shorts views per rolling 90 days** to receive Shorts Creator Pool revenue. A channel launched in Nov 2026 will almost certainly face the 2027 bar.

### Cited Findings
**YouTube Shorts length**
- [OFFICIAL, via search extract] Square or vertical videos up to **3 minutes** are categorised as Shorts. Google's page reportedly references videos uploaded after a set date; third parties say the change happened in **Oct 2024**, while one extract of the UK page cites "8 December 2025", a discrepancy to re-check — [YouTube Help, Understand three-minute YouTube Shorts](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/15424877?hl=en); [Riverside](https://riverside.com/blog/how-long-can-youtube-shorts-be)
- [OFFICIAL, via search extract] "Any Short over one minute in duration with an active Content ID claim of any type, including manual claims, will be blocked globally" — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/15424877?hl=en)
- [BLOG] Two sources say that from **~Sept 2026**, Shorts of 1–3 minutes with an active Content ID claim are **no longer auto-blocked**. This is **unconfirmed on Google's page** — [Argil](https://www.argil.ai/blog/how-long-can-youtube-shorts-be); [Postfa.st](https://postfa.st/sizes/youtube/shorts)
- [3P DATA] OverseerOS (16,537 Shorts, 1,137 channels; observational): **16–30 s** had the highest median views and the largest share above 100k views (~38%). The author says this is not causal — [OverseerOS](https://www.overseeros.com/blog/best-youtube-shorts-length)
- [3P DATA, older and secondhand] Inflow Network (5,400 Shorts): 50–60 s clips earned ~22× more views than clips under 10 s — [Bytecap](https://www.bytecap.io/research/best-youtube-shorts-length); [Piktochart](https://piktochart.com/blog/how-long-youtube-shorts/)
- [3P DATA] Metricool 2026 (799,718 videos): YouTube Shorts views up >127% year over year, versus +76% for long-form — [Tubefilter, 3 Aug 2026](https://www.tubefilter.com/2026/08/03/youtube-shorts-long-form-study-metricool/)
- No source tested <60 s vs 1–3 min Shorts head-to-head — [Bytecap](https://www.bytecap.io/research/best-youtube-shorts-length)

**YouTube Partner Program (current, Nov 2026)**
- [OFFICIAL, via secondary] **Fan-funding/Shopping tier:** 500 subscribers + 3 public uploads in 90 days + 3,000 public watch hours in 12 months **or 3M valid public Shorts views in 90 days**. **Ad-revenue tier:** 1,000 subscribers + 4,000 watch hours in 12 months **or 10M valid public Shorts views in 90 days** — [AIR Media-Tech](https://air.io/en/monetization/youtube-partner-program-requirements-2026-the-complete-guide); [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/pt/blog/post/youtube-partner-program-guide)
- [PRESS] YPP eligibility and Shorts revenue sharing use **engaged views**, not the post-March-2025 public view count — [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/youtube-changes-shorts-view-counts-no-change-to-monetization/543005/)

**YouTube Partner Program (announced 10 Aug 2026, effective 1 Feb 2027)**
- [OFFICIAL, via press] New applicants for ads/Premium revenue sharing will need **1,000 subscribers + 8,000 qualified watch hours in 365 days, or 1,000 subscribers + 20M qualified Shorts views in 90 days**. Existing members keep their status — [NewscastStudio](https://www.newscaststudio.com/2026/08/10/youtube-partner-program-monetization-changes-2027/); [TechTimes](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/323954/20260811/youtube-partner-program-doubles-entry-bar-adds-monthly-shorts-earnings-gate.htm); [Metricool](https://metricool.com/youtube-partner-program-2027-changes/)
- [OFFICIAL, via press] From **1 Feb 2027**, YPP channels must keep **10M qualified Shorts views in a rolling 90 days** to receive a share of the **Shorts Creator Pool** each month. Falling short pauses only Shorts pool revenue (long-form earnings and YPP membership continue) and resumes once back over the bar — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/youtube-cuts-shorts-pay-for-channels-under-10m-views-from-february-2027/); [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/youtube-updates-partner-program-terms-shorts-payout-rules/585331/)
- [PRESS] Updated monetization modules must be accepted in Studio by **31 Jan 2027**. YouTube says total creator investment is unchanged and it expects to pay more in 2027 than in 2026 — [Sebertech](https://sebertech.com/news/2026/08/12/youtube-updates-partner-program-terms-and-shorts-payout-rules-for-2027/); [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/youtube-updates-partner-program-terms-shorts-payout-rules/585331/)
- [PRESS/BLOG] Sources disagree on some details, such as whether existing members must meet the new entry thresholds, and **no source confirms the future of the 500-subscriber/3M-Shorts-views fan-funding tier after Feb 2027** — [Digital Trends](https://www.digitaltrends.com/movies/youtube-just-doubled-the-grind-before-creators-can-start-making-ad-money/); [OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/resources/youtube-partner-program-changes-2027/)
- [BLOG, anecdote] One monetized creator reports Shorts revenue of roughly $100 per 1M views. This is a single anecdote, not a dataset — [HookClone](https://www.hookclone.com/blog/how-many-views-on-youtube-shorts-is-good)

**TikTok Creator Rewards Program (CRP)**
- [BLOG, consistent across many] Eligibility: **18+** (19+ in South Korea), **10,000 followers**, **100,000 video views in the last 30 days**, a **personal account** in good standing (business accounts can't apply), an eligible country (commonly listed: US, UK, DE, FR, IT, ES, BR, MX, JP, KR), and **original videos longer than 1 minute** posted after joining. Shorter videos help reach thresholds but earn nothing — [Postlink](https://postlinkapp.com/blog/tiktok-creator-rewards-program); [Toptal](https://www.toptal.com/creator/post/how-to-join-the-tiktok-creator-rewards-program); [Gemlist](https://www.gemlist.io/blog/tiktok-creator-rewards-requirements)
- [BLOG] Payout estimates range from ~$0.40 to $2.00 per 1,000 qualified views; none are official. Some sources add an account-age minimum of 30 days or appeal windows; these are unconfirmed — [Multilogin](https://multilogin.com/blog/mobile/tiktok-creator-rewards-program/); [Elev8or](https://www.elev8or.io/blog/tiktok-creator-rewards-program-explained)

### Inferences
- **For the sub-60 s plan:** TikTok will be a **reach and audience platform, not a revenue one**, unless the creator later adds 61–90 s "deep-dive" variants once at 10k followers. If CRP is a goal, keep the TikTok account **Personal** (CRP requires it).
- On YouTube, a new channel's realistic path is the **500-sub fan-funding tier** (if it survives 2027) and long-term brand deals. Shorts ad revenue requires ~20M engaged views per 90 days for new applicants after 1 Feb 2027, which is a stretch goal for year one, not a 90-day goal.
- Keep YouTube Shorts **≤60 s** to stay clear of any Content ID auto-block risk if music is ever claimed, and because the length data favors 15–60 s. A deadpan "boring but useful" tip fits naturally at **20–45 s**.
- Consider an occasional long-form compilation (e.g., "50 boring but useful tips", 8–12 min). vidIQ data favors hybrid channels, and long-form watch hours are the alternative YPP route.

### Gaps
- Exact current TikTok CRP country list and terms (official creators.tiktok.com page not retrieved).
- TikTok upload length limits for 2026 (commonly cited: 10 min in-app, up to 60 min upload) were **not verified** this session.
- YouTube's Shorts revenue split (creator share of allocated pool, widely cited as 45%) was not verified this session.
- Whether the YPP 500-subscriber fan-funding tier changes on 1 Feb 2027 is unknown.

## 5. Monetization risk for AI-voiced, faceless channels (YouTube "inauthentic content", TikTok originality)

### Takeaway
Neither platform bans AI voices or faceless formats. What they demonetize is **mass-produced, templated, low-value content**. YouTube renamed "repetitious content" to **"inauthentic content" on 15 July 2025** and called it a minor clarification. TikTok's CRP excludes "unoriginal" content: reposts, minimally edited clips, slideshows and text-only videos. The biggest risk for a deadpan TTS-plus-vector channel is **sameness across uploads**: identical template, cadence and script structure, with no distinct perspective.

### Cited Findings
- [PRESS/BLOG] On **15 July 2025**, YouTube renamed its "repetitious content" monetization policy to **"inauthentic content"**, clarifying that mass-produced and repetitious content is ineligible to earn. Rene Ritchie (Head of Editorial & Creator Liaison) described it as a minor update formalizing existing enforcement against spammy, mass-produced uploads — [AlternativeTo](https://alternativeto.net/news/2025/7/youtube-updates-its-policy-to-demonetize-inauthentic-mass-produced-ai-generated-content); [Fliki](https://fliki.ai/blog/youtube-monetization-policy-2025)

Generated videos with an original script, a specific angle and real information are not targeted — [Fliki](https://fliki.ai/blog/youtube-monetization-policy-2025); [YTGrowth](https://ytgrowth.io/blog/youtube-ai-policy)
- [BLOG] Faceless channels with original scripts, real curation and a consistent style remain eligible. The risk is faceless **plus** templated output — [InVideo](https://invideo.io/blog/youtube-kills-ai-faceless-channels/); [Oakgen](https://oakgen.ai/blog/faceless-ai-channel-youtube-monetization-rules)

- [BLOG] AI "expert" personas giving health, legal, financial or political advice are the clearest at-risk category — [YTGrowth](https://ytgrowth.io/blog/youtube-ai-policy); [Faceless.my](https://faceless.my/youtube/youtube-inauthentic-content-policy-faceless-ai-2026/)

### Inferences
- **What "sufficient original value" looks like for this channel.** These are practical safeguards inferred from the policy intent, not official checklists:
  1. Original, researched scripts with a recognisable deadpan voice and a consistent "persona" written by a human.
  2. Bespoke vector animation per video, not the same stock loop with swapped text.
  3. Vary structure across episodes (myth vs fact, "useless superpower", step demo, odd stat).
  4. A fact-checked source on screen or in the description for odd facts.
  5. A recognisable but not cloned look.
- Avoid health, finance and legal "advice" framing delivered by the AI narrator. Keep tips to everyday life, tech and household items, or frame them as trivia, to stay out of the AI-persona sensitive-advice risk zone.
- Pick a distinctive (possibly custom or cloned-own) TTS voice and keep scripts human-written. A generic default TTS voice reading list-style text over static slides resembles the "slideshow/text-only" formats TikTok excludes.
- Do not produce 5+ near-identical uploads per day. Volume amplifies the "mass-produced" signal.

## 6. AI disclosure and labeling (YouTube "altered or synthetic", TikTok AIGC and C2PA)

### Takeaway
On YouTube, **clearly animated or unrealistic content and AI used for "production assistance" do not require the altered/synthetic disclosure**. A generic TTS narrator over vector animation is most likely exempt. A voice that imitates a real person, or realistic synthetic footage, would require it. On TikTok, the self-label is required for **realistic** AI content. TikTok also auto-labels via **C2PA Content Credentials** (since May 2024) and is adding **invisible watermarks** (testing since Nov 2025). Viewers can now dial down AI content in their feeds.

### Cited Findings
- [OFFICIAL] YouTube does not require disclosure for content that is "clearly unrealistic, animated, includes special effects, or has used generative AI for production assistance." Disclosure is aimed at realistic content a viewer could mistake for real people, places or events — [YouTube Blog, "How we're helping creators disclose altered or synthetic content"](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/disclosing-ai-generated-content/)
- [BLOG] Voice specifics: cloning **your own** voice (e.g., for dubbing) is described as exempt. A synthesized voice of **someone else**, or one making a real person appear to say something, requires disclosure. Sources conflict on generic AI voiceovers: some call them production assistance (no label), others list synthetic voices "resembling real people" as disclosable — [Versely](https://www.versely.studio/blog/youtube-ai-disclosure-exemptions-list); [Influencer Marketing Hub](https://influencermarketinghub.com/ai-disclosure-rules/); [ShortsFast](https://shortsfast.com/blog/youtube-ai-content-disclosure-rules-2026/)

One source says the label may now read "AI use" in Studio; this is unconfirmed — [Influencer Marketing Hub](https://influencermarketinghub.com/ai-disclosure-rules/); [Air.io](https://air.io/en/youtube-glossary/what-is-youtubes-ai-content-disclosure-policy)
- [BLOG] TikTok has read **C2PA Content Credentials** on upload and auto-labeled AI content since **May 2024**. Creators must still self-label realistic AI content, and TikTok can apply the label itself — [MakeInfluence](https://www.makeinfluence.com/en/academy/content-credentials-c2pa-how-platforms-label-ai-assisted-content); [Fastlane](https://www.usefastlane.ai/blog/do-you-have-to-label-ai-generated-content)

TikTok's watermark only travels on content that passed through TikTok AI tools or a credentialed source — [Noobclaw](https://noobclaw.com/blog/tiktok-invisible-watermark-2026/); [MakeInfluence](https://www.makeinfluence.com/en/academy/content-credentials-c2pa-how-platforms-label-ai-assisted-content)

### Inferences
- **YouTube:** For stylised vector animation plus a generic, non-impersonating TTS voice, the "altered content" answer is most likely **No**. If any realistic AI imagery or a voice resembling a real person is used, answer **Yes**, since there is no monetization penalty per secondary sources. When in doubt, disclose.
- **TikTok:** Check whether the TTS or animation tools embed C2PA credentials, since files may be auto-labeled. A mild AI label is not a policy violation, but viewers who dial down AI content may see the channel less. Being transparent ("AI voice" in bio) is a low-cost, trust-preserving choice.

## 7. "Made for kids" and minors: classification, teen restrictions, and 2025–2026 laws

### Takeaway
Animated, simple-looking educational shorts risk being treated as "made for kids" (MFK) on YouTube. That would strip comments, notifications, personalized ads and other features, and route the channel toward kids' content. Set the channel and each video to **"No, not made for kids"** and make the audience signals clearly teen/adult. Separately, reaching **under-18 viewers is getting harder**:
- **Australia** has banned under-16 accounts on YouTube, TikTok and others since **10 Dec 2025**.
- The **UK** has enforced Online Safety Act age checks since **25 July 2025**, and an under-16 ban was reportedly announced in June 2026.
- **YouTube's US AI age estimation** (from Aug 2025) applies teen protections, such as no personalized ads, to inferred under-18s.
- US state app-store age-verification laws are rolling in through 2026–2027.

### Cited Findings
**YouTube "made for kids"**
- [OFFICIAL, via help link and press] Creators must designate each video or channel as MFK or not, under COPPA. MFK videos get restricted features, including **comments and notifications**. **Personalized ads are removed** (contextual ads still run), and features like the miniplayer, download and add-to-playlist are disabled. MFK videos are more likely to be recommended alongside other kids' videos — [YouTube Help (yt.be)](https://yt.be/help/9Sgi); [Tubefilter, Jan 2020](https://tubefilter.com/2020/01/07/youtube-disables-more-product-features-kid-oriented-content/)
- [PRESS, older] YouTube's machine learning has been reported to mis-flag content as MFK (single outlet). Disabled comments hurt engagement — [Reclaim The Net](https://reclaimthenet.org/youtube-coppa-changes-violent-videos-made-for-kids/); [SocialRails](https://socialrails.com/blog/why-youtube-comments-turned-off)

**TikTok teen accounts**
- [OFFICIAL] TikTok: content created by users **under 16 is not eligible for the For You feed**. No one can Duet or Stitch under-16 users' videos. Downloads are off and locked for under-16s. Direct messaging is available only to accounts **16+** — [TikTok Transparency Center, Protecting teens](https://www.tiktok.com/transparency/en/protecting-teens/)
- [OFFICIAL, regulator] TikTok accounts of under-18s are private by default — [eSafety Commissioner, TikTok guide](https://www.esafety.gov.au/key-issues/esafety-guide/tiktok)

**Australia**
- [OFFICIAL, regulator] From **10 Dec 2025**, Australians under 16 can no longer access or create TikTok accounts — [eSafety Commissioner](https://www.esafety.gov.au/key-issues/esafety-guide/tiktok)
- [PRESS] About **4.7 million accounts** were deactivated or restricted across 10 covered platforms (Facebook, Instagram, Kick, Reddit, Snapchat, Threads, **TikTok**, X, **YouTube**, Twitch) by Jan 2026. Fines go up to A$49.5M — [WTOP/AP, Jan 2026](https://wtop.com/australia/2026/01/social-media-platforms-removed-4-7-million-accounts-after-australia-banned-them-for-children/)
- [PRESS] Meta removed 750k+ Australian under-16 accounts as of 30 June 2026. Research suggests more than 80% of under-16s were still using social media in the ban's first three months — [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/meta-756k-australian-teen-accounts-removed)

**UK**
- [OFFICIAL/legal] Online Safety Act child-protection duties, including "highly effective" age checks, came into force on **25 July 2025**. Ofcom's monitoring focuses on TikTok, YouTube and others — [Lewis Silkin](https://www.lewissilkin.com/insights/2025/07/24/online-age-checks-and-childrens-safety-measures-must-be-in-place-from-25-july-102kv96)
- [OFFICIAL] In March 2026, Ofcom told major platforms, including TikTok and YouTube, to enforce minimum-age rules with highly effective age checks — [Ofcom](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/online-safety/protecting-children/keep-underage-children-off-your-platforms-ofcom-tells-tech-firms)
- [PRESS] In July 2026, Ofcom opened an investigation into whether TikTok's UK age checks are effective — [ISPreview](https://www.ispreview.co.uk/index.php/2026/07/ofcom-investigate-tiktok-over-weak-uk-internet-age-verification-measures.html); [The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/news/uk/2026/07/16/tiktok-defends-its-age-verification-checks-as-uk-regulator-steps-up-pressure/)
- [LEGAL COMMENTARY, single source] On 15 June 2026, the UK PM reportedly announced an under-16 ban on platforms including TikTok and YouTube, with legislation intended "before Christmas" and Ofcom compliance in 2027. **Verify against government sources** — [Lawyer Monthly](https://www.lawyer-monthly.com/2026/06/online-safety-act-under-16-social-media-ban-ofcom-compliance-2027/)

**United States**
- [PRESS] YouTube began **AI age estimation** in the US on **13 Aug 2025**, starting with a small set of users. It infers under-18 status from activity and account history regardless of stated birthdate. Inferred teens get **personalized ads disabled** and digital-wellbeing tools turned on. Users can appeal with ID, credit card or selfie. Creators may see their audience shift toward teen-classified users — [CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/amp/news/youtube-ai-powered-technology-teen-users); [Variety](https://au.variety.com/2025/digital/news/youtube-ai-age-verification-automatically-restrict-under-18-users-26503); [Android Authority](https://www.androidauthority.com/youtube-age-estimation-us-rollout-3586621)
- [LEGAL] **Texas App Store Accountability Act (SB 2420)** was enjoined on 23 Dec 2025. The Fifth Circuit stayed the injunction in late May/early June 2026, so the law is in effect pending appeal. The Supreme Court declined to block it in July 2026; Fifth Circuit argument was held on 4 Aug 2026 — [Morrison Foerster](https://www.mofo.com/resources/insights/251111-texas-targets-app-stores-with-new-accountability-law); [Wiley](https://www.wiley.law/alert-Key-Developments-With-State-App-Store-Accountability-Acts-as-Texas-Act-Takes-Effect)

### Inferences
- **How to stay out of MFK (practical signals).** These follow COPPA-factor logic: subject matter, visual style, language and audience composition.
  - Set the channel default and every upload to "No, not made for kids".
  - Write for teens and adults: dry, ironic adult humour; topics like dorm, work, commuting, phone and money hacks, adulting, taxes, first apartment.
  - Avoid nursery-style colour palettes, cute mascots aimed at young kids, kid voices, toys, cartoons popular with kids, songs or rhymes, and "for kids" or "kids learn" phrasing in titles, tags and descriptions.
  - The deadpan adult TTS voice itself helps.
- **Audience mix matters for revenue.** Viewers inferred as under-18 (YouTube) receive no personalized ads, so a teen-heavy audience lowers RPM. Australia (and soon the UK) removes under-16s entirely. **Skew topics toward 16–29, especially 18–24** (college, first jobs, renting, adulting) while staying broadly teen-appropriate.
- These laws mostly affect *who* can have accounts and see personalized ads, not what an adult creator may post. An adult-run, non-MFK channel remains compliant.
- The TikTok "under-16 content not in For You feed" rule applies to the *creator's* age. The creator must be an adult account holder (and 18+ for CRP).

## 8. Music and copyright (TikTok Commercial Music Library, YouTube audio library and Content ID on Shorts)

### Takeaway

On YouTube, use the **YouTube Audio Library** or original/AI-generated music, and keep Shorts **≤60 s** if any third-party track is used. Shorts over 1 minute with an active Content ID claim have been **blocked globally** (a reported Sept 2026 relaxation is unverified). Cross-posting a file with trending TikTok audio baked in can be muted or claimed on other platforms.

### Cited Findings
- [BLOG, music vendors] Personal TikTok accounts can use the full sound library. **Business accounts are limited to the CML** because personal-entertainment licenses do not cover commercial use. TikTok reportedly tightened enforcement in its **July 2025 Music Terms** — [Soundstripe](https://www.soundstripe.com/blogs/why-can-i-only-use-commercial-sounds-on-tiktok); [Thematic](https://hellothematic.com/music-rules-tiktok-instagram-youtube/)
- [BLOG] Switching Business → Personal restores the full library but does not un-mute already-muted videos. Size estimates for the CML conflict (600k+ per TikTok's business blog, 1M+ per its help centre) — [Soundstripe](https://www.soundstripe.com/blogs/how-to-turn-off-commercial-sounds-on-tiktok); [SocialRevver](https://socialrevver.com/blog/tiktok-commercial-music-library)
- [OFFICIAL, via search extract] YouTube: a Short **over one minute with an active Content ID claim** (including manual claims) is **blocked globally**. YouTube Audio Library tracks will not receive a Content ID claim — [YouTube Help, three-minute Shorts](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/15424877?hl=en)
- [BLOG, unverified] A reported Sept 2026 change says 1–3 min Shorts with claims are no longer auto-blocked — [Argil](https://www.argil.ai/blog/how-long-can-youtube-shorts-be); [Postfa.st](https://postfa.st/sizes/youtube/shorts)

### Inferences
- **Simplest safe setup:** produce **original or AI-generated music** (e.g., the toolkit's ACE-Step `music_gen.py`, `lofi`/`corporate-bg` presets) or YouTube Audio Library tracks, mixed low under the TTS. Bake it into the master file so the identical clean file works on all three platforms with no claims. Add trending TikTok sounds natively in the TikTok app only (at very low volume) if desired, on a **Personal** account.
- Verify that the licence of any AI-music tool output permits commercial use on social platforms. This was not researched here.
- If the TikTok account is switched to Business for analytics or scheduling reasons, it loses the trending-sound library and CRP eligibility. Personal or Creator account type is the better default.

## 9. Realistic growth benchmarks (first 30–90 days) and what to track weekly

### Takeaway
The best dataset (vidIQ, July 2026) shows the **typical (median) first Short reaches ~1,362 views after 30 days**. Among channels that kept posting (≥10 Shorts in 90 days), **86.5% had at least one of their first ten Shorts pass 1,000 views within 30 days**. Everything else, such as "day-90 = 1k–10k views per Short" or "70% APV is good", comes from blogs with no methodology. Plan for **hundreds to low thousands of views per video in month one**, with high variance and an occasional outlier. Judge progress by **retention and swipe metrics trending up**, not by subscribers.

### Cited Findings
- [3P DATA] vidIQ "First 10 videos" study (448,413 channels starting early 2025, published July 2026): the typical first Short reached **1,362 views after 30 days**, versus 180 for the typical first long-form video. Among channels posting ≥10 Shorts in their first 90 days, **86.5%** had one of their first ten Shorts reach 1,000 views within 30 days. The sample is filtered to active channels — [vidIQ Research](https://vidiq.com/research/youtube-first-10-videos-study/)
- [BLOG] 50–500 views per Short in the first 48 hours is called "normal" for channels under 1,000 subscribers. Another guide calls 500–1,000 in the first 24 hours "solid" for a new Shorts channel — [HookClone](https://www.hookclone.com/blog/how-many-views-on-youtube-shorts-is-good); [Humble & Brag](https://humbleandbrag.com/blog/youtube-shorts-benchmarks)
- [BLOG, no methodology] A 90-day framework table: about 100–500 average views per Short and 50–200 subscribers at day 30; 500–2,000 views and 200–800 subscribers at day 60; 1,000–10,000 views and 1,000–5,000 subscribers at day 90 — [ClipForge](https://clip-forge.io/blog/youtube-shorts-channel-from-scratch-2026)
- [BLOG] "Healthy" Shorts average percentage viewed (APV) in 2026 is said to be ≥70%; another guide targets 80%+ — [Humble & Brag](https://humbleandbrag.com/blog/youtube-shorts-benchmarks); [HookClone](https://www.hookclone.com/blog/how-many-views-on-youtube-shorts-is-good)
- [BLOG] Viewed-vs-swiped thresholds (e.g., ~70% viewed) are rules of thumb, not official, and YouTube has published none — [Grow Creator](https://growcreator.pro/blog/viewed-vs-swiped-away-youtube-shorts); [ContentMatch](https://contentmatch.io/youtube-shorts-algorithm-viewed-vs-swiped-ratio-2026/)
- [3P DATA] Only 1–3% of TikTok videos reportedly reach "viral" status (third-party claim) — [Conbersa](https://www.conbersa.ai/learn/tiktok-recommendation-algorithm-2026)
- [3P DATA] Socialinsider has studied how long Shorts take to get views. Shorts often keep accruing views over days to weeks, which is a reason not to judge a Short in its first 24 hours — [Socialinsider](https://www.socialinsider.io/blog/how-long-does-it-take-for-yt-shorts-to-get-views/)

### Inferences
- **Realistic month-1 expectation (per platform):** most videos get 100–1,500 views; one or two may reach 5k–50k+ if the hook lands. **Total followers or subscribers in 30 days: tens to low hundreds** is normal; 1,000+ is a good outcome.
- **Realistic day-90 expectation:** the median Short is in the high hundreds to low thousands of views, with a few outliers driving most subscriber growth. A YPP Shorts threshold (10M, or 20M after Feb 2027, engaged views per 90 days) is **not** a realistic 90-day goal; the 500-subscriber tier might be.
- **Weekly scorecard (suggested; thresholds are folklore-grade starting targets, not official):**
  1. **YouTube viewed vs swiped away**: aim for a rising trend; ≥60–70% is commonly cited as strong.
  2. **APV / average view duration**: aim for ≥70–80% on 20–45 s videos; ≥100% means rewatches.
  3. **TikTok average watch time and "watched full video" %**: track from TikTok Analytics per video.
  4. **Shares and saves per 1,000 views**: the "useful tip" format should over-index on saves and shares; compare week over week.
  5. **Follows/subs per 1,000 views**: compare week over week; it shows whether the series identity converts.
  6. **Engaged views vs views (YouTube)**: tracks progress toward YPP.
  7. **Output consistency**: number of videos posted vs planned.
- Review the **3 best and 3 worst** videos each week by retention and swipe rate, and iterate on hooks and topics accordingly. This is more useful than chasing exact posting times.

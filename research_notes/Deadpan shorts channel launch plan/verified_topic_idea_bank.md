#### Pillar A — Everyday life skills & small hacks

**A1. The fuel-door arrow** (evergreen)
- Fact: Most newer cars have a small triangle or arrow next to the fuel-pump icon on the gauge, and it points to the side the fuel door is on — [Snopes](https://www.snopes.com/autos/techno/icon.asp); [Motor1](https://www.motor1.com/features/804101/what-fuel-gauge-arrow-means/)
- Why: The arrow is credited to Ford engineer Jim Moylan and first appeared on the 1989 Ford Escort and Mercury Tracer — [SlashGear](https://www.slashgear.com/1868551/gas-tank-which-side-identify-easy-way)
- How: Look at the fuel gauge. Arrow left = fuel door on the left; arrow right = fuel door on the right — [Motor1](https://www.motor1.com/features/804101/what-fuel-gauge-arrow-means/)
- Caveats: Not every car has it (Jalopnik names MINI as an exception) — [Jalopnik](https://jalopnik.com/1685497291). The viral version that says "the side the pump *handle* is on" is wrong, because the handle placement means nothing — [Snopes](https://www.snopes.com/autos/techno/icon.asp). Say "left/right", not "driver/passenger side", because Motor1's driver-side framing is US-centric and reverses in right-hand-drive countries (inference).

**A2. Why pen caps have a hole** (evergreen)
- Fact: The hole in many pen caps, such as the BIC Cristal, is there so air can still pass if someone inhales the cap. It is a choking-safety feature tied to the ISO 11540 standard — [Snopes](https://www.snopes.com/fact-check/holes-pen-caps-prevent-choking/); [Mental Floss](https://www.mentalfloss.com/article/545682/surprising-reason-why-pen-caps-have-tiny-holes-top)
- Why: In the UK, nine children died choking on pen caps between 1970 and 1984. ISO 11540 covers writing instruments likely to be used by children up to age 14; it was first implemented in 1993 and revised in 2014 and 2021 — [ScienceAlert](https://www.sciencealert.com/why-there-are-holes-in-tops-of-pen-caps-lids-bic); [Mental Floss](https://www.mentalfloss.com/article/545682/surprising-reason-why-pen-caps-have-tiny-holes-top)
- How (the angle): A cap can comply with a hole, with several holes, or by being large enough (over 16 mm) that it can't be swallowed. The separate hole in the barrel equalizes pressure so ink flows and doesn't leak — [Mental Floss](https://www.mentalfloss.com/article/545682/surprising-reason-why-pen-caps-have-tiny-holes-top)
- Caveats: The hole is a safety margin, not a guarantee. A UK 13-year-old died after inhaling a pen cap in 2007 — [Mental Floss](https://www.mentalfloss.com/article/545682/surprising-reason-why-pen-caps-have-tiny-holes-top). Sources disagree on when BIC first added the hole (around 1990–1991) — [Snopes](https://www.snopes.com/fact-check/holes-pen-caps-prevent-choking/). The script should say "don't chew pen caps", not "it's safe now".

**A3. Don't pour grease down the drain** (evergreen; spikes around holiday cooking)
- Fact: Fats, oils and grease solidify inside pipes and are a leading cause of sewer blockages. The EPA, as quoted by Clemson Extension, says grease is "the most common cause (47%) of reported blockages" — [Clemson HGIC](https://hgic.clemson.edu/factsheet/f-o-g-fats-oils-and-grease-pollution/)
- Why: Hot water and dish soap only push the grease further down the pipe, where it cools and coats the inside. Garbage disposals don't stop grease either — [Arlington County, VA](https://www.arlingtonva.us/Government/Programs/Water-Utilities/Sewer/FOG); [Clemson HGIC](https://hgic.clemson.edu/factsheet/f-o-g-fats-oils-and-grease-pollution/)
- How: Pour or scrape grease into a can or jar, let it cool or freeze, then put it in the trash. Don't flush "flushable" wipes, because they don't break down — [Clemson HGIC](https://hgic.clemson.edu/factsheet/f-o-g-fats-oils-and-grease-pollution/); [Arlington County](https://www.arlingtonva.us/Government/Programs/Water-Utilities/Sewer/FOG)
- Scope: The 47% figure is US (EPA), quoted second-hand. The disposal advice applies everywhere, but local rules differ (inference).

**A4. Never mix bleach with other cleaners** (evergreen)
- Fact: Bleach plus ammonia produces toxic chloramine gases, and bleach plus any acid, including vinegar, releases chlorine gas — [Washington State Dept. of Health](https://www.doh.wa.gov/YouandYourFamily/HealthyHome/Contaminants/BleachMixingDangers); [NJ Dept. of Health](https://www.nj.gov/health/workplacehealthandsafety/occupational-health-surveillance/Hazard_Alert-Mixing-Cleaners.pdf)
- Why: Ammonia is in more products than people expect, including some glass cleaners and some toilet-bowl and pet-stain products, and urine contains it too — [Health Canada](https://recalls-rappels.canada.ca/en/alert-recall/reduce-your-risk-toxic-gases-never-mix-bleach-other-cleaning-products); [WA DOH](https://www.doh.wa.gov/YouandYourFamily/HealthyHome/Contaminants/BleachMixingDangers)
- How: Use one product at a time, follow the label, and ventilate. If exposed, get fresh air and contact poison control — [Health Canada](https://recalls-rappels.canada.ca/en/alert-recall/reduce-your-risk-toxic-gases-never-mix-bleach-other-cleaning-products)
- Pairs well with "unclogging a drain safely": don't stack chemical drain cleaners or follow a cleaner with bleach (inference from the same sources). No authoritative "best way to unclog a drain" source was found, so that topic is not in the bank (see Gaps).

**A5. Reading a clothing care label** (evergreen; back-to-school / moving-out)
- Fact: In the US, the FTC Care Labeling Rule (1971) requires clothing to carry instructions for at least one safe care method, and since July 1997 symbols may replace words — [FTC, "Clothes Captioning"](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/clothes-captioning-complying-care-labeling-rule)
- Why it's confusing: The US-approved symbols (ASTM) are "similar, but not identical" to the international ISO symbols used elsewhere — [FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/clothes-captioning-complying-care-labeling-rule)
- How: Animate the basic symbol families (wash tub, bleach triangle, drying square, iron, dry-clean circle) only after checking them against the FTC guide's symbol chart. The search extracts did not show the chart itself (see Gaps).
- Caveats: Region-specific (US ASTM vs ISO/GINETEX in UK and EU). The rule's legal status is uncertain: the FTC proposed repealing it in 2020 — [Federal Register notice](https://downloads.regulations.gov/FTC-2020-0058-0001/content.htm). Don't claim "the law requires…" on screen without checking the current eCFR (16 CFR 423).

**A6. Unit price, the small number on the shelf tag** (evergreen; Black Friday, groceries)
- Fact: The unit price (price per ounce, per 100 g, per count) lets you compare different package sizes, and NIST publishes a best-practice unit-pricing guide (SP 1181, updated June 2025) — [NIST](https://www.nist.gov/news-events/news/2015/02/new-best-practices-guide-shows-unit-pricing-great-deal); [NIST SP 1181](https://www.nist.gov/document/sp1181-unit-pricing-guidepdf)
- Why: Bigger isn't always cheaper per unit. Purdue researchers reported in 2024 that 82% of consumers use unit pricing when it's available, according to NIST's summary — [NIST](https://www.nist.gov/node/393126)
- How: Compare the unit price, not the sticker price, and make sure both tags use the same unit (per oz vs per lb). Consumer Reports notes unit pricing can be confusing because units vary — [Consumer Reports](https://www.consumerreports.org/cro/magazine/2015/06/unit-pricing-can-be-confusing/index.htm)
- Caveat: The US has no federal mandate for unit pricing; rules vary by state — [Consumer Reports](https://www.consumerreports.org/cro/magazine/2015/06/unit-pricing-can-be-confusing/index.htm). The UK and EU have their own rules (not researched).

**A7. Wet phone? Don't use rice** (tech crossover; also a myth episode, see Q4)
- Fact: Apple says not to put a wet iPhone in a bag of rice, because small rice particles can damage it — [Apple Support 102643](https://support.apple.com/en-me/102643); [Euronews](https://www.euronews.com/next/2024/02/21/heres-what-to-do-if-your-iphone-gets-wet-hint-dont-put-it-in-rice)
- How (Apple's steps): Tap the phone gently against your hand with the connector facing down, leave it somewhere dry with some airflow, and try charging after at least 30 minutes. Don't use heat, compressed air, cotton swabs or paper towels — [Apple Support](https://support.apple.com/en-me/102643); [PetaPixel](https://petapixel.com/2024/02/20/apple-says-stop-putting-your-wet-iphone-in-rice)
- Scope: The Apple doc is about the iPhone "liquid detected" alert (Lightning or USB-C). It's sensible for other phones too, but those makers weren't checked (inference).

**A8. Clipboard history on Windows (Win + V)** is filed under Tech (D1), and the padlock-hole topic was **dropped** (see Gaps).

#### Pillar B — Money & adult basics (mostly US; label it)

**B1. APR vs interest rate**
- Fact: The interest rate is the cost of borrowing the money. APR adds the loan's fees, so it is the broader measure and usually higher than the interest rate on a mortgage — [CFPB (mortgage APR)](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/what-is-the-difference-between-a-mortgage-interest-rate-and-an-apr-en-135/); [CFPB (interest rate)](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/what-is-a-loan-interest-rate-en-733/)
- Why: APR lets you compare loans whose fees differ. The CFPB also explains why payday-loan APRs look huge — [CFPB (payday APR)](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/what-is-an-annual-percentage-rate-apr-and-why-is-it-higher-than-the-interest-rate-for-my-payday-loan-en-1625/)
- How: When comparing two loans, compare APR to APR.
- Caveats: On credit cards the APR and interest rate are often the same number. On adjustable-rate mortgages, the APR does not show the maximum rate — [CFPB](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/what-is-the-difference-between-a-mortgage-interest-rate-and-an-apr-en-135/). US terminology; the UK uses "APR" with different rules (not researched).

**B2. The 50/30/20 budget**
- Fact: The rule comes from Elizabeth Warren and Amelia Warren Tyagi's book *All Your Worth* (2005). It splits **after-tax** income into roughly 50% needs, 30% wants and 20% savings or debt payoff — [SoFi](https://www.sofi.com/learn/content/50-30-20-budget/); [Acorns](https://www.acorns.com/learn/saving/50-30-20-budget-rule/)
- Caveats: It's a rule of thumb. High rent or student loans may force a different split — [SoFi](https://www.sofi.com/learn/content/50-30-20-budget/). Sources list the book as 2005 (one says 2006). No government source "endorses" it, so present it as one popular framework, not official advice.

**B3. Compound interest, explained in one sentence**
- Fact: Compound interest is interest earned on your original money plus the interest it has already earned. The St. Louis Fed's example (using the Investor.gov calculator) has $5,000 at 8% growing to about $79,840 after 36 years — [St. Louis Fed](https://www.stlouisfed.org/open-vault/2018/september/how-compound-interest-works)
- The Rule of 72 (72 ÷ rate ≈ years to double; reasonably accurate between about 2% and 10%) is a good companion line, but it was confirmed only by secondary sources — [Nasdaq](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/the-rule-of-72-explained:-how-long-will-it-take-to-double-your-savings)
- Caveat (must say on screen): The example is for illustration, returns are not guaranteed, and it isn't investment advice. Compounding works the same way *against* you on debt (inference; ties to B6).

**B4. What actually makes up a credit score (FICO)**
- Fact: FICO's published weights are payment history 35%, amounts owed 30%, length of credit history 15%, new credit 10% and credit mix 10% — [myFICO](https://www.myfico.com/crediteducation/whatsinyourscore.aspx)
- Why: Payment history dominates. A payment 30+ days late counts as a delinquency, and late marks can stay on a report for up to seven years, with their impact fading over time — [Experian](https://www.experian.com/blogs/ask-experian/what-factor-has-the-biggest-impact-on-credit-score)
- How: Pay on time (autopay at least the minimum) and keep balances low relative to limits (inference from the 35%/30% weights).
- Caveat: US only. FICO is one scoring model and others (e.g., VantageScore) weight things differently (inference; not researched). The UK and other countries use different systems.

**B5. You can check your credit report free every week**
- Fact: All three US credit bureaus have **permanently** extended free weekly credit reports at AnnualCreditReport.com — [FTC consumer alert, Oct 2023](https://consumer.ftc.gov/consumer-alerts/2023/10/you-now-have-permanent-access-free-weekly-credit-reports)
- How: Use AnnualCreditReport.com (the only site the FTC recognizes), or call 1-877-322-8228 — [FTC](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/free-credit-reports)
- Caveats: Look-alike sites may charge you or steal your data. AnnualCreditReport.com won't email you asking for your SSN — [FTC](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/free-credit-reports). A credit report is not a credit score (inference). US only.

**B6. The box on your credit-card statement nobody reads**
- Fact: US card statements must include a "Minimum Payment Warning" showing how long payoff takes and the total cost if you pay only the minimum, along with the monthly payment needed to clear the balance in 3 years. The rule is Regulation Z (12 CFR 1026.7(b)(12)) under the CARD Act — [CFPB model form G-18(D)](https://files.consumerfinance.gov/f/documents/cfpb_credit-card-penalty-fees_model-forms_g-18-d.pdf); [GetOutOfDebt.org](https://getoutofdebt.org/264444/they-said-paying-the-minimum-is-fine-the-warning-box-on-your-own-statement-says-otherwise)
- How: Find the box on your statement and compare the "minimum only" row with the "3-year" row.
- Caveat: The CFPB sample is from 2012, so real layouts vary. Check the wording against the current Reg Z text. US only.

**B7. Reading a US payslip: why about 7.65% disappears**
- Fact: Employees pay 6.2% Social Security tax and 1.45% Medicare tax (FICA), 7.65% in total, and employers match it — [IRS Topic 751](https://irs.gov/taxtopics/tc751)
- Caveats: Social Security tax stops above an annual wage cap, and there is an extra 0.9% Medicare tax on high earners (above $200,000) — [IRS Topic 751](https://irs.gov/taxtopics/tc751); [Patriot Software](https://www.patriotsoftware.com/blog/payroll/what-small-businesses-should-know-about-fica/). The cap figure differs between secondary sources, so verify it with the SSA or IRS before showing a number. Federal and state income tax withholding are separate lines (inference). A UK payslip version (PAYE/NI) was **not researched**.

**B8. Your W-2 is due by January 31** (seasonal: January)
- Fact: US employers must give employees their Form W-2 copies by January 31 — [IRS](https://irs.gov/newsroom/irs-reminds-employers-of-january-31-deadline-for-form-w-2-other-wage-statements)
- Caveat: The IRS pages found are 2022–2023 notices. Confirm the 2027 date (31 Jan 2027 is a Sunday; the weekend roll-over rule came only from a vendor source) — [Paycor](https://www.paycor.com/resource-center/articles/w2-form/)

#### Pillar C — Study, brain & focus (research-backed only)

**C1. Practice testing (active recall) beats rereading**
- Fact: The big 2013 review of 10 study techniques (Dunlosky, Rawson, Marsh, Nathan, Willingham; *Psychological Science in the Public Interest*) rated **practice testing** as one of only two "high utility" techniques — [APS press release](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/news/releases/which-study-strategies-make-the-grade.html); [DOI 10.1177/1529100612453266](https://doi.org/10.1177/1529100612453266)
- Why: In Roediger & Karpicke (2006), restudying won on a test 5 minutes later, but testing won on tests 2 days and 1 week later — [Roediger & Karpicke 2006 (PDF)](https://psychology.ecu.edu/wp-content/pv-uploads/sites/216/2019/03/Roediger-Karpicke-2006.pdf); [Vanderbilt CFT](https://cft.vanderbilt.edu/?p=22452)
- How: Close the notes, write or say everything you remember, then check. Use flashcards or past papers.
- Caveat: A widely shared figure (≈61% vs ≈42% recall at one week) comes from a secondary source, so check it in the paper before putting it on screen.

**C2. Spacing (distributed practice)**
- Fact: The same 2013 review rated distributed practice, meaning spreading study sessions over time, as the other "high utility" technique — [APS](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/news/releases/which-study-strategies-make-the-grade.html)
- How: Split the same total hours across several days and review earlier material in later sessions (inference from the definition).

**C3. Highlighting and rereading rated "low utility"**
- Fact: Dunlosky et al. rated highlighting, rereading, summarization, the keyword mnemonic and imagery for text as **low utility**, even though most students rely on rereading and highlighting — [APS](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/news/releases/which-study-strategies-make-the-grade.html)
- Caveat: "Low utility" doesn't mean useless. Say "researchers rated it low", not "it does nothing".

**C4. Interleaving, self-explanation and "asking why"**
- Fact: Interleaved practice (mixing problem types), self-explanation and elaborative interrogation (asking "why is this true?") were rated **moderate** utility — [APS](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/news/releases/which-study-strategies-make-the-grade.html)
- Caveat: The authors note these techniques help students who are motivated and able to use them; they're not a cure-all — [APS](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/news/releases/which-study-strategies-make-the-grade.html)

**C5. Sleep is part of studying (why all-nighters backfire)**
- Fact: A 2021 *Psychological Bulletin* meta-analysis (Newbury, Crowley, Rastle, Tamminen) covering studies from 1970 to 2020 found that total sleep deprivation hurts memory both **before** learning (g = 0.62) and **after** learning (g = 0.28) — [Newbury et al. 2021 (PMC)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8893218/)
- Why: Sleep after learning helps consolidate new memories. An NIH "Sleep On It" article quotes researcher Robert Stickgold: the night after you study matters at least as much as the night before — [NIH (mirror; verify at newsinhealth.nih.gov)](https://testdomain3.nih.gov/node/1481)
- Caveats: The effect after learning is modest. The authors flag publication bias and low statistical power and call for replication — [Reviewer.ly abstract](https://biomed.reviewer.ly/app/publication/bb17b062-b080-4d47-9f16-f0411192a04c); [Rastle Lab](https://www.rastlelab.com/post/sleep-deprivation-impairs-learning-and-memory-but-now-is-the-time-for-replication-science). Don't use the viral "40% less learning" claim unless you can confirm it on the official NIH page. Keep this about study habits, not sleep medicine.

**C6. Pomodoro: a timer, not a memory technique** (good for an honest, deadpan episode)
- Fact: The Pomodoro technique isn't among the techniques Dunlosky et al. rated. A 2023 study (Biwer et al., *British Journal of Educational Psychology*) compared fixed short breaks (Pomodoro-like) with self-chosen breaks, and secondary summaries **disagree** on whether fixed breaks raised or lowered concentration and motivation. Reported differences were mostly in how students felt, not in output — [DOI 10.1111/bjep.12593](https://doi.org/10.1111/bjep.12593); [Preprints.org summary](https://www.preprints.org/manuscript/202503.0845)
- Framing: "Fine if it helps you start. The evidence that it improves learning is thin." Don't claim it "boosts focus by X%".

#### Pillar D — Tech & phone tricks (flag version-dependent items)

**D1. Win + V = clipboard history (Windows 10/11)**
- Fact: Win + V opens clipboard history. It's **off by default**: turn it on from the Win + V prompt or in Settings > System > Clipboard — [Microsoft Support](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/help/12445/windows-keyboard-shortcuts)
- Caveat: The "last 25 items" figure is from third-party sources, not Microsoft — [WTOP / Data Doctors](https://wtop.com/tech/2024/02/data-doctors-useful-windows-keyboard-shortcuts)

**D2. Win + Shift + S = screenshot part of the screen**
- Fact: Win + Shift + S starts a screen capture with the Snipping Tool overlay — [Microsoft Support](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/help/12445/windows-keyboard-shortcuts); [Digital Citizen](https://www.digitalcitizen.life/clipboard-history-windows-11-shortcut/)
- Mac equivalent (Cmd + Shift + 4) was not verified in this session.

**D3. Ctrl + Shift + T reopens the tab you just closed**
- Fact: In Chrome, Ctrl + Shift + T (Cmd + Shift + T on Mac) reopens recently closed tabs in the order they were closed. If you last closed a window, it reopens the window — [Google Chrome shortcuts](https://support.google.com/a/answer/3001912?hl=en); [How-To Geek](https://www.howtogeek.com/446304/how-to-reopen-a-closed-tab-in-google-chrome/)
- Caveat: It doesn't bring back Incognito tabs — [How-To Geek](https://www.howtogeek.com/446304/how-to-reopen-a-closed-tab-in-google-chrome/). Firefox and Edge support the same shortcut according to the reporting (not verified with those vendors).

**D4. Incognito doesn't make you invisible**
- Fact: Google says Incognito activity may still be visible to the websites you visit, your employer or school (whoever runs the network) and your internet service provider. Signing in lets a site identify you — [Google Chrome Help](https://support.google.com/chrome/answer/9845881?hl=en)
- Why: Incognito only stops *your device* from saving history and cookies after you close the window — [Mozilla](https://www.mozilla.org/en-US/technology/incognito-browser)
- Angle: It's a "the next person on this laptop won't see it" mode.

**D5. Swiping away apps doesn't save battery (iPhone)**
- Fact: Apple's guidance is to force-close an app only if it's unresponsive. Apple's Craig Federighi reportedly answered "No and no" when asked whether he force-quits apps and whether doing so helps battery — [Daring Fireball](https://daringfireball.net/2017/07/you_should_not_force_quit_apps); [TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/phones/iphone/does-closing-apps-on-your-iphone-save-battery-life-the-surprising-answer-is-no-heres-why)
- Why: iOS suspends background apps, and relaunching an app from scratch costs more energy than resuming it — [TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/phones/iphone/does-closing-apps-on-your-iphone-save-battery-life-the-surprising-answer-is-no-heres-why)
- Caveat: iOS-specific. Apps using Background App Refresh still do work, so the battery fix there is Settings, not swiping (inference from the same sources). The Android equivalent wasn't checked.

**D6. iOS 26 "Adaptive Power" (version- and model-specific)**
- Fact: iOS 26 adds Adaptive Power (Settings > Battery > Power Mode), which makes small performance adjustments to save battery and switches on Low Power Mode at 20% — [AppleInsider](https://appleinsider.com/articles/25/06/10/ios-26-iphone-battery-overhaul-trends-and-adaptive-power); [SlashGear](https://www.slashgear.com/1885210/ios-26-adaptive-power-mode-save-iphone-battery)
- Scope: It's on by default on the iPhone 17 family and opt-in on other supported models. Eligibility is tied to Apple Intelligence-capable phones (about iPhone 15 Pro and later), but sources disagree on the exact list — [9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2025/09/15/psa-iphone-17-comes-with-a-special-new-battery-mode-enabled/)
- Caveat: Only secondary coverage was found, so confirm against Apple's support article before naming models. Put "iOS 26+ / supported models" on screen.

**D7. Android: Adaptive Battery and Battery Saver (Pixel paths)**
- Fact: On Pixel phones, Adaptive Battery (Settings > Battery > Battery Saver > Adaptive Battery) makes apps you use less often run less in the background. Battery Saver can be set to turn on automatically at a chosen percentage — [Google Pixel Help](https://support.google.com/pixelphone/answer/7015477?hl=en); [Pixel battery tips](https://support.google.com/pixelphone/answer/6090612?hl=en)
- Android 16 adds a "Battery health" menu on recent Pixels (Pixel 6/6a and newer; sources conflict) — [9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/?p=676202)
- Caveat: Menu paths differ on Samsung and other brands. Show "Pixel, Android 16" on screen.

**D8. Two-factor: app or passkey beats SMS codes**
- Fact: CISA ranks phishing-resistant MFA (FIDO/WebAuthn, i.e. passkeys and security keys) as strongest and SMS one-time codes as weakest. Any MFA is still better than a password alone — [CISA training page](https://cisa.gov/resources-tools/training/why-strong-password-isnt-enough-your-guide-multifactor-authentication); [CISA fact sheet](https://cisa.gov/sites/default/files/publications/fact-sheet-implementing-phishing-resistant-mfa-508c.pdf)
- How: Turn on 2FA for email first, then banking and social accounts. Prefer a passkey or authenticator app over text codes where the service offers them (inference from the CISA ranking).
- Caveat: The quote "Do not use SMS as a second factor" (Dec 2024) is reported second-hand. Paraphrase it rather than quoting.

**D9. Spotting phishing in 3 seconds**
- Fact: FTC red flags: unexpected messages asking you to "confirm" personal or financial information, invoices you don't recognize, and links to pay or fix an account problem — [FTC](https://consumer.ftc.gov/consumer-alerts/2024/12/phishing-scams-can-be-hard-spot); [FTC handout (PDF)](https://www.consumer.ftc.gov/sites/www.consumer.ftc.gov/files/articles/design-files/how_to_recognize_and_avoid_phishing_scams_tl-508.pdf)
- How: Don't click. Contact the company through a number or website you already know is real, then report the message (US: ReportFraud.ftc.gov) — [FTC](https://consumer.ftc.gov/consumer-alerts/2024/12/phishing-scams-can-be-hard-spot)

**D10. "Missed delivery" texts are a scam** (seasonal: late Nov–Dec, though they run all year)
- Fact: The FTC warns about fake shipping-notification texts and emails that ask you to click a link to reschedule a delivery. Scammers send them all year, not only at the holidays — [FTC, Dec 2023](https://consumer.ftc.gov/consumer-alerts/2023/12/fake-shipping-notification-emails-text-messages-what-you-need-know-holiday-season)
- How: Don't click. Check tracking in the retailer's or carrier's own app or site — [FTC](https://consumer.ftc.gov/consumer-alerts/2023/12/fake-shipping-notification-emails-text-messages-what-you-need-know-holiday-season)
- Caveat: Forwarding to 7726 (SPAM) is common US carrier practice, but the source found was a blog. Verify it with a carrier or the FCC before saying it.

**D11. Emergency SOS on iPhone**
- Fact: On Face ID iPhones (and iPhone SE 2nd gen / 8), press and hold the side button and either volume button until the sliders appear and the countdown ends, and the phone calls emergency services. A rapid five-press of the side button is also an option — [Apple Support 102262](https://support.apple.com/102262); [Apple personal-safety guide](https://support.apple.com/en-gu/guide/personal-safety/ips4f0cd709b/web)
- Caveats: Steps vary by model, region and settings, so check yours. The Android equivalent wasn't verified in this session (see Gaps). Never demo an actual call; show the countdown being cancelled (inference).

**D12. Wet phone / rice** (see A7).

#### Pillar E — Odd-but-true everyday science ("why is it like that")

**E1. Why airplane windows are round**
- Fact: Rounded windows spread cabin-pressure stress evenly. The square-cornered windows on the 1950s de Havilland Comet concentrated stress at the corners, which was linked to metal fatigue and fatal in-flight breakups — [Mental Floss](https://mentalfloss.com/article/84603/why-are-airplane-windows-round); [HowStuffWorks](https://science.howstuffworks.com/transport/flight/modern/airplane-windows-round.htm)
- Caveat: Sources disagree on crash dates (1953/1954). Don't put exact dates on screen unless you check the official Comet inquiry. These are secondary sources.

**E2. The tiny hole in airplane windows**
- Fact: The small "breather" or "bleed" hole in the middle pane equalizes pressure so the outer pane carries the load, and it also lets moisture out so the window doesn't fog or frost — [BGR](https://bgr.com/2155765/why-airplane-windows-have-tiny-holes/); [NZ Herald](https://nzherald.co.nz/travel/reason-for-mysterious-tiny-hole-on-aeroplane-windows/27GNS3466ZB2VMD25CBNZWLDUQ)
- Caveat: Only secondary journalism was found; the manufacturer source is a gap. Avoid the Alibaba "product-insights" pages, which contradict each other.

**E3. Why onions make you cry**
- Fact: Cutting an onion triggers a chemical reaction that releases an irritant gas, syn-propanethial-S-oxide, which stings your eyes — [Library of Congress, Everyday Mysteries](https://www.loc.gov/everyday-mysteries/food-and-nutrition/item/why-does-chopping-an-onion-make-you-cry/)
- How: Use a sharp knife, because blunt knives burst more cells and release more irritant — [Live Science](https://www.livescience.com/32299-why-does-slicing-onions-make-me-cry.html)

**E4. Why QWERTY (and the myth about it)**
- Fact: The popular story that QWERTY was designed to slow typists down is disputed. Kyoto University researchers (Yasuoka & Yasuoka) argue the layout evolved in response to early users, especially telegraph operators. QWERTY stuck because of Remington and the 1893 Union Typewriter Company — [Smithsonian Magazine](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/arts-culture/fact-of-fiction-the-legend-of-the-qwerty-keyboard-49863249/)
- Caveat: Historians still argue about this. Frame it as "the real story is messier than the meme", not "the meme is 100% false".

**E5. What the numbers on a barcode mean**
- Fact: A 12-digit UPC is a company prefix (assigned through GS1) + an item number + a final **check digit** calculated from the other digits to catch scanning errors — [GTIN.info](https://www.gtin.info/upc/); [barcode.graphics](https://www.barcode.graphics/gtin-12/)
- Twist: The first digits do **not** show where a product was made. They show which GS1 member organization issued the number — [GS1 Support](https://support.gs1.org/support/solutions/articles/43000734188-does-the-gs1-prefix-first-3-digits-of-the-ean-13-barcode-number-show-the-country-of-origin); [PolitiFact](https://www.politifact.com/factchecks/2020/apr/23/viral-image/barcode-wont-tell-you-if-product-was-made-china/)
- Caveat: The structure details come from barcode vendors and gtin.info; the "not country of origin" point comes straight from GS1. Avoid political examples like the "729 prefix" boycott claims; Full Fact covers them, but they're divisive for a deadpan channel — [Full Fact](https://fullfact.org/online/israel-barcode-numbers-repeat/)

**E6. Why fingers wrinkle in water**
- Fact: Finger wrinkling is an active, nerve-controlled response in which blood vessels under the skin narrow; it isn't just skin swelling — [Live Science](https://www.livescience.com/26097-why-fingers-pruney-water.html); [Nature News](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature.2013.12175)
- Why (likely): In a 2013 Newcastle University study (Kareklas, Nettle, Smulders; *Biology Letters*), people with wrinkled fingers moved wet objects faster, about 12% faster, but dry objects no faster, consistent with a "tyre tread" grip effect — [Live Science](https://www.livescience.com/26097-why-fingers-pruney-water.html); [Nature News](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature.2013.12175)
- Caveat: Researchers say the evolutionary reason isn't settled. Use "probably" or "one leading theory" on screen.

**E7. Why the sky is blue (and not because of the ocean)**
- Fact: Air molecules scatter shorter (blue) wavelengths of sunlight much more than red (Rayleigh scattering), and the sky isn't reflecting the ocean — [NASA Space Place](https://spaceplace.nasa.gov/blue-sky/en/); [Royal Museums Greenwich](https://www.rmg.co.uk/discover/explore/why-is-the-sky-blue)
- Bonus: Sunsets look red because the light passes through more air — [NOAA SciJinks](https://scijinks.gov/about/k-12-education/atmosphere/why-the-sky-blue)
- Hook is low because it's well known (see Q2). Use it as filler or pair it with "Mars has the opposite sunset".

**E8. Pen cap hole / fuel arrow** also fit this pillar (see A1–A2).

#### Pillar F — Safety basics, calmly framed (officially sourced only)

**F1. Grease fire in a pan**
- Fact: For a small grease fire, slide a lid over the pan, turn off the burner, don't move the pan, and leave the lid on until the pan cools — [US Fire Administration](https://www.usfa.fema.gov/gallery/pictographs/pictograph34.html)
- Why: Never throw water on a grease fire — [USFA (archived tip sheet)](https://webarchive.library.unt.edu/eot2008/20080917192508mp_/http:/www.usfa.dhs.gov/media/quick_response/ffwf-11.shtm). Flour, sugar and baking powder can make it worse. Baking soda only works on very small fires and takes a lot, and Minnesota fire chiefs say people panic and grab the wrong white powder, so they recommend a lid — [Minnesota State Fire Chiefs (PDF)](https://www.ighmn.gov/DocumentCenter/View/13784/MSFCA-Recipe-Book-for-Fire-Safety); [Illinois Extension](https://extension.illinois.edu/food-safety/kitchen-fires)
- Escalation: If it isn't out right away or has spread beyond the pan, get out and call the fire department — [Houston Fire Dept.](https://houstontx.gov/fire/safetyinfo/cookingsafety.html); [Illinois Extension](https://extension.illinois.edu/food-safety/kitchen-fires)
- Oven fire: Keep the door closed and turn off the heat — [Illinois Extension](https://extension.illinois.edu/food-safety/kitchen-fires)

**F2. The recovery position**
- Fact: The recovery position is for someone who is unconscious but **breathing normally**. Kneel beside them, put the near arm out at a right angle (palm up), place the back of their other hand against their cheek, bend the far knee to a right angle, then roll them toward you onto their side — [NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/first-aid/recovery-position/); [MedlinePlus](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/presentations/100211_1.htm)
- Caveats: Call emergency services first (999 in the UK) — [St John Ambulance Cymru](https://www.sjacymru.org.uk/en/page/recovery-position). Suspected spinal injury calls for a different technique. Put "Take a first-aid course" on screen.

**F3. Emergency numbers differ, but 112 is a good backup on mobiles**
- Fact: 112 is the EU-wide emergency number (since 1991), free from landlines and mobiles. It's also used in some non-EU countries and is "available worldwide on GSM mobile networks" — [European Commission](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/node/182/printable/pdf)
- Why: The UK uses 999, Australia 000, New Zealand 111, the US 911. EENA says 112 and 911 are recognized as emergency calls on any mobile device — [EENA](https://eena.org/blog/is-112-a-valid-emergency-number-in-america/); [FCC](https://www.fcc.gov/news-events/blog/2010/12/01/emergencies-abroad-what-do-you-dial)
- Caveat: Routing varies by country and network, so learn the local number when you travel. Never say "112 works everywhere".

**F4. Burns: 20 minutes of cool running water**
- Fact: NHS guidance is to cool a burn under cool or lukewarm running water for 20 minutes, remove nearby clothing and jewellery unless stuck, and not use ice, iced water, butter or other greasy substances (some NHS guidance also says no toothpaste) — [NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/burns-and-scalds/treatment/); [NHS 111 Wales](https://111.wales.nhs.uk/burnsandscalds/); [LSEBN/NHS guideline (PDF)](https://www.qvh.nhs.uk/wp-content/uploads/2016/07/LSEBN-Burns-First-Aid-Guideline.pdf)
- Caveat: Get medical help for serious burns, large burns, burns on children, or when in doubt (see NHS page for criteria).

**F5. Nosebleed: lean forward, not back**
- Fact: Sit down, lean forward, breathe through your mouth, and firmly pinch the soft part of the nose just above the nostrils for 10–15 minutes — [nidirect (NHS NI)](https://www.nidirect.gov.uk/conditions/nosebleed); [Healthdirect Australia](https://www.healthdirect.gov.au/amp/article/nosebleed)
- Why: Leaning forward lets the blood drain out instead of down your throat — [nidirect](https://www.nidirect.gov.uk/conditions/nosebleed)
- Caveat: Don't pinch the bony bridge — [Healthdirect](https://www.healthdirect.gov.au/amp/article/nosebleed). Seek care if it won't stop (thresholds on the linked pages).

**F6. Lightning: "When thunder roars, go indoors"**
- Fact: If you can hear thunder you're likely within striking distance. Get inside a fully enclosed building or a hard-topped metal vehicle, and wait 30 minutes after the last thunder before going back out — [NWS lightning safety](https://www.weather.gov/safety/lightning-safety-overview); [NWS one-pager (PDF)](https://www.weather.gov/media/owlie/LightningSafety-OnePager-11-29-18.pdf)
- Twist: A car protects you because of its metal roof and sides, **not** its rubber tyres — [NOAA lightning myths (archived)](https://webarchive.library.unt.edu/web/20130213213517/http:/www.lightningsafety.noaa.gov/myths.htm)

**F7. Carbon monoxide** (seasonal: winter)
- Fact: CO poisoning risk rises in winter as heating runs longer. Install battery or battery-backup CO alarms near sleeping areas; never run generators, cars or other engines indoors or in garages; never heat a home with a gas oven — [CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/nceh/features/copoisoning); [CDC PSA toolkit](https://www.cdc.gov/natural-disasters/psa-toolkit/avoiding-carbon-monoxide-poisoning.html); [CPSC](https://www.cpsc.gov/Newsroom/News-Releases/2023/Winter-is-Coming-Keep-Your-Family-Safe-from-Carbon-Monoxide-Poisoning-and-Fires)
- Symptoms: headache, dizziness, weakness, nausea, confusion. Get to fresh air and call 911 — [CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/nceh/features/copoisoning)
- Caveat: Some CDC pages found were archived; check current wording and death statistics before using numbers.

**F8. The 3-foot rule for space heaters** (seasonal: winter)
- Fact: NFPA guidance is to keep anything that can burn at least 3 feet (1 m) from heating equipment, turn space heaters off when you leave the room or go to sleep, and use a heater with tip-over auto shut-off — [PIRG (citing NFPA)](https://pirg.org/edfund/resources/space-heaters-lead-to-1700-fires-and-80-deaths-a-year-here-are-tips-to-stay-safe/)
- Why: Nearly half of heating-equipment fires (2014–2018) happened in December, January and February — [PIRG (citing NFPA)](https://pirg.org/edfund/resources/space-heaters-lead-to-1700-fires-and-80-deaths-a-year-here-are-tips-to-stay-safe/)
- Caveat: NFPA's own page wasn't reached, so cite nfpa.org directly once you've checked it.

**F9. Smoke alarms: test monthly, replace at 10 years**
- Fact: Test smoke alarms monthly with the test button and replace the whole unit every 10 years — [NFPA Fire Prevention Week tip sheet (via York County)](https://www.yorkcounty.gov/DocumentCenter/View/58510/FPW24_Theme-Tip-Sheet-FLS)
- Why 10 years: The CPSC says the 10-year replacement period balances safety and cost, at roughly a 30% chance of failure before replacement — [CPSC (PDF)](https://www.cpsc.gov/s3fs-public/smokealarm_0.pdf)

**F10. Hands-Only CPR (teens/adults who suddenly collapse)**
- Fact: The AHA's two steps: call 911, then push hard and fast in the centre of the chest at 100–120 compressions per minute, to the beat of "Stayin' Alive" — [American Heart Association](https://www.heart.org/en/news/2023/02/01/11-things-to-know-to-save-a-life-with-cpr); [AHA (2018 rate update)](https://www.heart.org/en/news/2018/07/23/new-resuscitation-guidelines-update-cpr-chest-pushes)
- Caveats: This applies to adults and teens who suddenly collapse. The depth figure (about 2 inches / 5 cm) was confirmed only second-hand for the 2025 guidelines. Show "Learn CPR properly: take a class", and use the AHA infographic as the visual reference — [AHA infographic (PDF)](https://www.heart.org/-/media/Files/Nation-of-Lifesavers/How-to-Perform-CPR-Infographic.pdf?sc_lang=en). Outside the US, use the local resuscitation council's wording (e.g., Resuscitation Council UK; not researched).

**F11. Never mix bleach** (see A4).

The 50/30/20 split is a guideline — [SoFi](https://www.sofi.com/learn/content/50-30-20-budget/). APR doesn't show the maximum rate on adjustable loans — [CFPB](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/what-is-the-difference-between-a-mortgage-interest-rate-and-an-apr-en-135/).
- **Study (C5/C6):** Sleep-deprivation effects are real but modest after learning, and the literature has publication-bias concerns — [Newbury et al. 2021](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8893218/); [Rastle Lab](https://www.rastlelab.com/post/sleep-deprivation-impairs-learning-and-memory-but-now-is-the-time-for-replication-science).
- **Pen cap (A2):** The hole reduces risk but doesn't remove it — [Mental Floss](https://www.mentalfloss.com/article/545682/surprising-reason-why-pen-caps-have-tiny-holes-top).

### Inferences
- Standard lower-third templates to build once in Remotion:
  - SAFETY: "General info from [NHS / AHA / USFA]. Emergency? Call 911 / 999 / 112. Take a first-aid course."
  - MONEY: "US-specific. Educational, not financial advice. Numbers are examples."
  - TECH: "Tested on: iOS 26 / Android 16 (Pixel) / Windows 11. Menus vary."
  - SOURCE: "Source: [agency] — link in description/comments."
- Avoid showing real brand logos in ways that imply endorsement (e.g., FICO, Apple), and avoid political barcode examples (E5).
- For the jellyfish myth (Q4), give only "don't use urine; follow lifeguard or local first-aid guidance". Evidence on what *does* work (vinegar) is mixed and species-dependent.

## Q4. Which popular viral "life hacks" or "facts" are false or unsafe, and could each become a "myth vs fact" episode?

### Takeaway
19 debunkable myths have credible sources. The best episodes are the ones where the myth is *actively harmful* (water on a grease fire, butter on burns, head back for a nosebleed, mixing cleaners, rice for a wet phone) or *universally believed* (10% of the brain, learning styles, the five-second rule, knuckle cracking).

### Cited Findings
1. **"Put a wet phone in rice."** False per Apple, since rice particles can damage the phone. **Episode: yes (3/3)** — [Apple Support](https://support.apple.com/en-me/102643); [PetaPixel](https://petapixel.com/2024/02/20/apple-says-stop-putting-your-wet-iphone-in-rice)
2. **"Throw water on a grease fire" / use flour.** Dangerous: never use water; flour, sugar and baking powder can worsen it; use a lid. **Episode: yes (3/3)** — [USFA](https://www.usfa.fema.gov/gallery/pictographs/pictograph34.html); [Minnesota State Fire Chiefs](https://www.ighmn.gov/DocumentCenter/View/13784/MSFCA-Recipe-Book-for-Fire-Safety)
3. **"Butter (or ice, or toothpaste) on a burn."** False. Use cool running water for 20 minutes. **Episode: yes (3/3)** — [NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/burns-and-scalds/treatment/); [LSEBN/NHS](https://www.qvh.nhs.uk/wp-content/uploads/2016/07/LSEBN-Burns-First-Aid-Guideline.pdf)
4. **"Tilt your head back for a nosebleed."** False. Lean forward and pinch the soft part. **Episode: yes (3/3)** — [nidirect (NHS)](https://www.nidirect.gov.uk/conditions/nosebleed)
5. **"Mix cleaners for extra strength" (bleach + vinegar or ammonia).** Dangerous: it makes chlorine or chloramine gas. **Episode: yes (3/3)** — [WA DOH](https://www.doh.wa.gov/YouandYourFamily/HealthyHome/Contaminants/BleachMixingDangers); [Snopes (Dawn + bleach)](https://www.snopes.com/fact-check/dawn-clorox-lethal/)
6. **"The five-second rule."** False as stated. Rutgers (Schaffner) found bacteria can transfer in under one second. Wetter food picks up more, and longer contact means more transfer. **Episode: yes (3/3)** — [Food Safety Magazine](https://www.foodsafetymagazine.com/news/the-5-second-rule-is-bogus-say-rutgers-researchers/)
7. **"Pee on a jellyfish sting."** False. Urine can make it worse, and vinegar works for many (not all) species. **Episode: maybe (2/3)**, because it needs careful medical framing — [Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/fact-or-fiction-urinating/); [Cleveland Clinic](https://health.clevelandclinic.org/pee-jellyfish-sting)
8. **"Closing all your apps saves battery" (iPhone).** False per Apple. **Episode: yes (3/3)** — [Daring Fireball](https://daringfireball.net/2017/07/you_should_not_force_quit_apps); [TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/phones/iphone/does-closing-apps-on-your-iphone-save-battery-life-the-surprising-answer-is-no-heres-why)
9. **"Incognito means nobody can see what I browse."** False per Google. **Episode: yes (3/3)** — [Google Chrome Help](https://support.google.com/chrome/answer/9845881?hl=en)
10. **"The first barcode digits tell you the country of origin."** False per GS1. **Episode: yes (3/3)**; avoid political examples — [GS1 Support](https://support.gs1.org/support/solutions/articles/43000734188-does-the-gs1-prefix-first-3-digits-of-the-ean-13-barcode-number-show-the-country-of-origin); [PolitiFact](https://www.politifact.com/factchecks/2020/apr/23/viral-image/barcode-wont-tell-you-if-product-was-made-china/); [GS1 UK](https://www.gs1uk.org/our-industries/news/2016/04/08/debunking-the-myth)
11. **"The pump-handle side of the fuel icon shows the tank side."** False; it's the arrow. **Episode: yes**, paired with A1 — [Snopes](https://www.snopes.com/autos/techno/icon.asp)
12. **"We only use 10% of our brains."** False. **Episode: yes (2/3; well known but evergreen)** — [Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/do-people-only-use-10-percent-of-their-brains/)
13. **"I'm a visual/auditory learner, so I should study that way" (learning styles / meshing).** No adequate evidence base, per Pashler et al. 2008. **Episode: yes (3/3; fits the study pillar)** — [APS](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/news/releases/learning-styles-debunked-there-is-no-evidence-supporting-auditory-and-visual-learning-psychologists-say.html)
14. **"Highlighting and rereading is good studying."** Rated low utility. **Episode: yes**, paired with C1/C3 — [APS](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/news/releases/which-study-strategies-make-the-grade.html)
15. **"Cracking your knuckles gives you arthritis."** Probably not, per Harvard Health, though habitual cracking may be linked to reduced grip strength. **Episode: yes (2/3)** — [Harvard Health](https://www.health.harvard.edu/pain/does-knuckle-cracking-cause-arthritis); [Harvard Health blog](https://www.health.harvard.edu/blog/knuckle-cracking-annoying-and-harmful-or-just-annoying-2018051413797)
16. **"Shaving makes hair grow back thicker or darker."** False per Mayo Clinic; the blunt tip just feels coarser. **Episode: yes (2/3)** — [Mayo Clinic](https://mayoclinic.org/healthy-lifestyle/adult-health/expert-answers/hair-removal/faq-20058427)
17. **"Lightning never strikes the same place twice" / "rubber tyres protect you."** Both false per NOAA/NWS. The Empire State Building is hit about 23 times a year, and a car's metal shell protects you, not its tyres. **Episode: yes (2/3; summer)** — [NOAA lightning myths (archived)](https://webarchive.library.unt.edu/web/20130213213517/http:/www.lightningsafety.noaa.gov/myths.htm)
18. **"QWERTY was designed to slow typists down."** Disputed; the telegraph-operator account is a strong alternative. **Episode: yes, as "it's complicated" (2/3)** — [Smithsonian](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/arts-culture/fact-of-fiction-the-legend-of-the-qwerty-keyboard-49863249/)
19. **"Pen caps have holes so the ink doesn't dry out."** The hole is for choking safety (ISO 11540); the barrel hole handles pressure. **Episode: yes**, paired with A2 — [Snopes](https://www.snopes.com/fact-check/holes-pen-caps-prevent-choking/)

### Inferences
- A recurring "Myth / Fact / Source" end card fits the brand: deadpan VO says "Myth." [beat] "No." and the source logo name appears.
- Episodes 2–5 double as safety education, which is high value but needs the Q3 safety caveat.
- Item 18 (QWERTY) and item 15 (knuckles) are the most nuanced. Script them as "mostly false" or "it's complicated" so the channel doesn't overstate a debunk.

### Gaps
- Not searched, so not included: "wait 30 minutes after eating to swim", "goldfish 3-second memory", "you lose most heat through your head", "Great Wall visible from space", "charging overnight ruins your battery". Each needs a source before use.
- Snopes, Reuters and AP Fact Check pages weren't fetched directly; most debunks rest on agency, vendor or primary-research sources instead.

### Cited Findings
